Pre-registration: the call-rate floor, replaced by what it stood in for
=======================================================================

Written 12 November 2026 against 5.52.1, after the survey below and BEFORE any change is built.

WHAT THE SURVEY FOUND (audit/local/nocall-survey; every array on tesla, 1,122 files, 1,001 unique)
  The floor (QC_CALL_FLOOR, call rate under 0.40) fires on none: the lowest call rate is 0.421.
  An array with no DNA does not stop calling. The 7 arrays recorded as having none (GSE148488's
  failed array and its "chr6p fragment only" cell; the laboratory's four "failed, no gDNA" cells and
  S23-12, "1,2,9,15,19 only") call 42% to 75% of markers, read 58% to 67% heterozygous, and are all
  refused already, as no genotype signal (information ratio 0.60 to 1.37). On a chromosome with no
  copy (Table S3's 21 nullisomies, and GSM4552418's 21 absent chromosomes) the array still calls
  26% to 61%; what marks it is log R at the no-calls (-1.7 to -2.2) and allele balance at the no-calls
  scattered (60% to 84% intermediate, against 0% to 20% on a present chromosome).
  So the floor's premise, that a failed reaction drives heterozygosity toward zero and imitates one
  genome, is the opposite of what failed arrays do here. The case it stood in for does exist, at high
  call rate: GSM4552364, a chaotic diploid cell (15 chromosomes disomic by its alleles), is staged
  haploid at 88% called, which no call-rate floor reaches.

THE CHANGE
  1. The floor is removed. Arrays without DNA stay refused by the information ratio.
  2. Where a parent is loaded, measured or reconstructed, the one-parent copy state supplies the
     ploidy the genotypes cannot. A haploid product carries one parent's genome on every chromosome;
     a diploid carries a second genome on most, through allele dropout, since dropout removes alleles
     at random and leaves the other parent's alleles in the calls. So:
       two genomes  disomic on at least half the chromosomes stating a copy state: never haploid
       one genome   the commonest state is one copy of one parent, on at least NORMAL_MAJORITY_MIN of
                    them: haploid, including where drop-in left the ploidy unknown
     Read inside the stage, so one reconstructed parent and two measured parents give one answer.

PREDICTIONS
  E1  On the 1,001 arrays read without a parent, no stage changes.
  E2  GSE148488, against the measured father, the eight-pronucleus rebuild, and both parents:
      no Table S3 cell staged haploid (GSM4552364 moves to a diploid rung); all 15 pronuclei staged
      haploid, including the four drop-in paternal ones now staged with ploidy unknown; the stage
      identical between the rebuild and both parents on 31 of 31.
  E3  S23, each cell against S23-00 and against the rebuild from the other 17: stage identical on 18
      of 18; the 13 pronuclei (sheet P or M, staging haploid today) staged haploid; S23-08, -16 and -17
      diploid; S23-12 and -15 refused.
  E4  Synthetic failure on real arrays (allele dropout and locus dropout over a grid, the no-call
      signal drawn as measured on nullisomic chromosomes): with a parent loaded, no diploid source is
      staged haploid at any allele dropout under 0.9; every haploid source not refused is staged
      haploid, including at call rates under 0.40. Without a parent, diploid sources staged haploid
      are reported against the floor's count on the same grid, as the cost of removing it.
  E5  The Zuccaro census and the laboratory's 95 arrays: every claim count unchanged except where
      E2 moves a stage.

A failed prediction is reported as a failure.
