THE REAL EVENT THAT WAS LOST, AND WHY THE OLD GATE HAD IT RIGHT BY LUCK.

GSM4472422 carries a hand-called maternal trisomy 15. Its share says maternal at 6.8 of that
cell's own share spreads, which is not in doubt. Its intensity reads +0.32 log2, which cleared
the old absolute floor. But that cell's per-chromosome intensity is wide: its 19 hand-normal
chromosomes run -0.18 to +0.22, so +0.32 is 1.8 of its own spreads and chromosomes 17, 19 and
22 sit at +0.18 to +0.22 with nothing wrong with them. Under the old floor those three would
have passed the intensity test as readily; they were not called only because their share did
not move. So the intensity in that cell cannot confirm an extra copy, the old gate was passing
noise, and the trisomy now leaves as an imbalance carrying both numbers and naming no parent.

That is a loss of information, not only of a false positive: the share names the parent clearly
and the gate discards it. A likelihood over the pair, which weighs a decisive share against a
weak intensity instead of refusing on either alone, is the resolution and is below.

Five of the 22 wrong-direction calls carry a posterior at or above 0.99. The posterior trades
sensitivity against them; it does not separate them. The reason is not the model.

IT IS THE ARM'S OWN BASELINE INTENSITY, WHICH ONE CELL CANNOT MEASURE

Each arm carries two builds at the same fraction, a gain and a loss, which solve together for the
baseline intensity B the untouched chromosome already read and for the chip's compression k. The
recovered k is 0.503, against the 0.55 to 1.0 grid, so the construction is honest about intensity.

  arms that produced a wrong-direction call   B = -0.151 +0.178 +0.280 +0.351 +0.370 +0.400
                                                  +0.406 +0.488
  arms that never did              (22 arms)  B median +0.025, range -0.047 to +0.099

The separation is total. A loss offset of k log2(1.7/2) = -0.13 on a chromosome already reading
+0.28 leaves it positive, and the five copy-number states then prefer the gain. The cell's own
intensity spread does not reveal the offset, which is why such a call can carry a posterior of
1.00 and why no threshold on that posterior reaches it. Within a single cell a per-chromosome
intensity baseline is not separable from a real copy-number change by intensity alone.

WHAT IS ROBUST TO IT: THE TILT

An extra copy from one parent and a missing copy from the other are the same tilt and move the
allele share the same way. A wrong direction therefore flips the named parent too, and the two
errors cancel.

  pair named a copy-number state on       267 of 300 builds
  tilt right                              262 of 267
    at f = 0.3                              94 of 98
    at f = 0.5                             109 of 110
    at f = 1.0                              59 of 59

Only 2 of the 22 wrong-direction calls named the right parent, and 20 of those 22 still named the
right tilt. So the direction is the fragile statement and the tilt is not, because the tilt comes
from the alleles. callPair reports it separately, as the posterior summed over the two states that
agree on it, and gainPair.check.ts holds the three-baseline case that demonstrates the cancellation.

THE SPLIT

Done on 840 builds over 19 cells, 10 to tuning and 9 held out, by accession. Both thresholds were
picked on the tuning half by rules fixed beforehand and applied unchanged to the other half.

  tilt at 0.75        named 287 of 450 held-out builds, wrong 4 (1.4%) against a 1 percent bar
  direction at 0.995  named 121 of 450 held-out builds, wrong 1 (0.8%) against a 5 percent bar

The direction is now read as a call at 0.995. The tilt missed its bar by 0.4 of a point and stays
reported beside each row rather than deciding the parent. At a third of the cells, where the
shipped thresholds detect nothing, the tilt is named on 77 of 180 held-out builds and right on 74.

The tuning half had zero wrong tilts at 0.75 and the held-out half had four, so the tuning half
alone would have reported a perfect tilt. That gap is what the split was for.

audit/gain-origin-split-preregistration.txt holds the rules, both outcomes, and the single read of
the real published events.
