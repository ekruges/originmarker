The other parent's copy on a segment, with one parent loaded
=============================================================
Scored on 15 November 2026 on open arrays only. Follows audit/truth/rebuilt-parent-check-2026-11-15.txt.
The table below is printed by audit/truth/parity.py from the run records.

WHAT WAS MISSING
----------------
The absence scan reads the loaded parent's own alleles, so with one parent a stretch missing the
OTHER parent's copy reads present throughout. On the 31 Table S3 cells, 20 segment losses are stated:
13 of the father's copy and 7 of the mother's. With the father alone 6.1.0 printed 13 of the 13 and
none of the 7; with both parents, 13 and 5.

THE FIRST RULE, AND WHY IT FAILED
---------------------------------
Scan the markers where the loaded parent is homozygous and the allele it lacks is common among
founders (0.5 or over), with the existing window scan. Result: 0 of 7. Those markers come at about 11
per megabase, a sixteenth of what the window scan is built for, so no window reaches its minimum.

THE EXPERIMENT BEFORE THE SECOND RULE
-------------------------------------
Offline, the 31 cells in three arms: measured father, father rebuilt from his pronuclei, mother alone.

  Every marker counted by how likely the other parent is to show there. Where the loaded parent is
  homozygous the sample shows the allele it lacks at b + a x q, q the founders' frequency of that
  allele; where it is heterozygous the sample is heterozygous at h. b, a and h are fitted on the
  array's own chromosomes that carry both parents. With the other parent's copy gone the rate is b.

  Scored as independent calls, noisy arrays gave runs on normal chromosomes as high as real losses
  on clean ones. Calls on amplified cells fail in clusters. Dividing each array's score by the
  variance of its own 500-marker block sums over the binomial variance (median over blocks) took
  the 954 readings of stated-normal chromosomes against the father, measured and rebuilt, to a
  maximum of 8.0, leaving two regions aside. The stated losses of the other parent's copy read 27 to
  525 with a good parental array.

  The two regions left aside read 34 to 168 on chromosomes the table states normal. The two-parent
  run prints a maternal loss on both, from the mother's own alleles.

  Against the mother alone, 477 readings: five over 8 and the rest 7.2 or under. Two of the five are
  where the two-parent run prints a paternal loss from the father's own alleles. One fails the 3%
  bound of rule 4. One is a 6 Mb stretch at the end of chromosome 4, read 50, where intensity falls
  by half a unit and two other cells of the embryo read the father's allele absent over the same
  stretch; the table states it normal. One, read 35, was not examined.

  Runs under 3 Mb reach 40 on normal chromosomes after the division: the lactase region, HLA, dense
  probe clusters, and one run of 2.4 Mb against the rebuilt father. Where both parents carry the
  same haplotype the sample shows no allele the loaded parent lacks, as it does where the other
  parent's copy is gone. Nothing in one parent's alleles separates the two.

THE RULE, written before it was built or run in the tool
--------------------------------------------------------
  1. Runs with exactly one parent loaded, its genome shown to be in the array, founder frequencies
     in the panel, and two or more autosomes read as carrying both parents by the one-parent copy
     state. Those autosomes are the reference and the chromosomes scanned.
  2. Rates b, a, h from the reference chromosomes: a straight-line fit per chromosome, median over
     chromosomes.
  3. A run's score is the summed log-likelihood ratio, gone against present, divided by the array's
     clustering, which is never taken under 1.
  4. Reported where the divided score is 15 or more, the run spans 3 Mb or more, and under 3% of the
     homozygous calls at the loaded parent's homozygous markers inside it are the homozygote that
     parent cannot give (the existing bound for the loaded parent's own copy being there).
  5. 15 is 1.9 times the highest score on the normal chromosomes of the experiment and 1.8 times
     under the weakest stated loss reached, the placement of the existing scan's threshold. 3 Mb is
     the smallest hand-called event and above every shared-haplotype run seen.
  6. Nothing changes with both parents loaded, or none.

Pass test: father alone, measured and rebuilt, finds the mother's segment losses on at least as many
as both parents do (5 of 7); stated-normal chromosomes left alone on at least as many as with both
parents (446 of 477); whole losses, the father's segment losses and named parents no worse than
6.1.0; both-parent and no-parent results identical to 6.1.0; the suite no worse on any line.

Written beside the rule, before the run: the fifth loss the two-parent run finds is about 2.5 Mb by
the alleles, so the rule was expected to find it against the rebuilt father, where the run reads
3.3 Mb, and to miss it against the measured father, where it reads 2.6 Mb.

THREE FAULTS THE FIRST RUN SHOWED
---------------------------------
270 rows: the 31 cells in three arms, 18 further arrays with a father, 133 unrelated arrays against
the rebuilt father. 22 new calls. 20 sat on a stated loss of the mother's copy or on a place the
two-parent run prints one. The other two:

  A haploid product, read against a parent rebuilt from haploid products, got one call. Such a
  sample carries one of the parent's two haplotypes in blocks; where it carries the one the rebuild
  did not take it shows alleles the rebuild lacks, and those blocks read as carrying both parents.
  Change: the scan runs only on a genome read as two parents', the guard the parent naming already
  uses.

  A cell with a stated paternal duplication got "maternal alleles absent" across the duplicated
  piece. An extra copy of the loaded parent's outnumbers the other parent's alleles and they drop
  out of the calls. Change: a run is not reported where intensity reads a gain over half of it or
  more.

  The third was read off the page, on one of the cells. The run log named the FATHER as the absent
  parent on the two segments the page had just printed as the mother's. The one-parent naming that
  ran on every segment counts opposite homozygotes at every marker where the loaded parent is
  homozygous, including markers where no founder carries another allele, where an opposite
  homozygote can only be a miscall. On a stretch at one copy those reach 5%, over its 3% bound, and
  against the measured father it named the father on all 8 new segments. Change: a segment the scan
  found is named by the scan and is not put to that naming, as a segment found against a second
  parent's own alleles is not.

THE STATED LIMIT
----------------
Ruled on 15 November: with one parent and one cell, a loss of the other parent's copy under 3 Mb is
not reported. A run of 1 Mb to 3 Mb that passes the score and the 3% bound is withheld, recorded with
its place, and named in the sample's limits; 1 Mb is the tool's floor for any segment.

On the 270 rows, 9 runs are withheld. One is the 2.5 Mb stated loss against the measured father.
Three are pieces of 2.4 and 2.8 Mb of another stated loss of the mother's copy, in a cell that has
lost most of its chromosomes and where no arm prints it. Two are one 1.6 Mb stretch on chromosome 7
stated normal in two cells, among the shared stretches of the experiment. Three are on two
laboratory cells with no stated answer. Counting what is printed or withheld, the mother's segment
losses are 6 of 7 against the measured father and 6 of 7 against the rebuilt one, and 5 of 7 with
both parents. Every other record is identical to the run before the change.

OTHER CELLS OF THE EMBRYO, TESTED AND NOT USED
----------------------------------------------
Cells of one embryo carry the same two parental genomes. A stretch the parents share then reads as
missing in every one of them, and a loss in one cell does not, so another cell showing the other
parent's alleles across a short run would settle it.

The premise was measured on 25 arrays of three embryos of one couple. At markers where the father
is homozygous and one cell shows the allele he lacks, does the other cell show it, scored in blocks
of 500 against that cell's own rates. Between clean arrays of one embryo, 78% to 100% of blocks read
the same haplotype (84 pairs, two embryos); between clean arrays of different embryos, 2% to 70%
(126 pairs). Opposite homozygotes between two arrays, in 20 Mb windows, say the same: 1% to 7% of
windows over 3% within an embryo, 41% to 62% between embryos.

The embryo carrying the 2.5 Mb loss does not hold to it. Its ten arrays do not read as one genome:
the cell with the loss against the cleanest other cell reads 36% to 43% of blocks the same, and two
other cells of it differ as siblings do (57% of windows over 3%). In the stretch itself four of its
cells show the mother's alleles (log-likelihood ratios of 330 to 600 for present), but a cell that
may carry a different maternal haplotype there cannot vouch for this one. No rule was built on it.

WHAT THE SCAN CANNOT SAY
------------------------
  Between related parents a shared haplotype can run past 3 Mb, and a run is then true of the
  alleles and not of the copy number.
  With one parent and one cell, a loss of the other parent's copy under 3 Mb is not reported.
  Two stated losses of the mother's copy sit in cells that have lost most of their chromosomes; no
  arm finds them, two parents included.
