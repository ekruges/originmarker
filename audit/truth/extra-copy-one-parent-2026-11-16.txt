Which parent gave an extra chromosome, with one parent loaded
=============================================================
Written 16 November 2026 BEFORE the code below is built or run. Open arrays only. Plan item 3.0d.
The three experiments behind it are recorded outside the repository (per-array tables stay under
audit/local/); this file carries the rule, the pass tests, the prediction and, after the run, the
scored result.

WHAT WAS MISSING
----------------
With both parents the tool names the parent of a constructed gain on 37 of 60 (92 of 158 held out).
With one rebuilt parent, 26 of 60 (57 of 158). With one measured parent the suite had no arm; offline
it names 40 of 60. Three causes on the 60:
  1. A gain whose one-parent copy state crosses into a loss state (an extra copy of the loaded parent
     puts its share at 2/3 of the disomic level, of the other parent's at 4/3) is never put to the
     gain test, and a lost parent is printed on it instead: 3 of the 60 in each one-parent arm.
  2. A rebuilt parent's homozygotes include true heterozygotes all of whose pronuclei showed one
     allele; they dilute the share. Offline this costs about 7 of the 60 at the orientation bar.
  3. The share is a mean over markers where the other parent did or did not pass the allele, so it
     carries that parent's genotype sampling. Where the sample is heterozygous the allele's share is
     the copy ratio itself, but only its upward movement belongs to one parent alone.

THE RULE
--------
1. One parent loaded, its genome shown to be in the array, the genome read as two parents'. A
   chromosome the intensity scan calls a gain is never reported as missing a parent's copy, whatever
   its copy state reads. It is scored as a chromosome carrying both parents and goes to the gain
   test. Why: a lost copy cannot raise intensity.
2. Rebuilt parent only. In the gain test's share, a marker where the rebuilt parent reads homozygous
   is weighted by the chance that it really is: (1-q)^2 / ((1-q)^2 + 2q(1-q) x 2^-m), q the founder
   frequency of the allele it lacks, m the fewest pronuclei a marker needs in the rebuild (stated by
   the rebuild's own banner; 5 for the suite's rebuild). A measured parent has weight 1.
3. A second route, for the OTHER parent only. Where 1 and 2 name no parent and intensity calls the
   gain: at the loaded parent's homozygous markers where the allele it lacks is common (0.5 or more)
   and the sample is called heterozygous (at least 100 markers), the median share of that allele is
   compared with the same median on the cell's other two-parent chromosomes (at least 6). Three
   spreads or more above them (spread never under 0.02) names the other parent's extra copy. Upward
   only: two copies of an allele the loaded parent does not carry can only come from the other
   parent, however the extra copy arose. Downward is not used: an extra copy of the loaded parent,
   and an extra copy made of both of the other parent's homologues, fall the same way.
4. Both parents loaded, or none: nothing changes. A chromosome intensity does not call a gain:
   nothing changes.
5. Every constant is one the tool already has, except m.

Not adopted: finding a gain that intensity does not call from the share and a weaker intensity bar
(adds 1 of 60 offline; its bar rests on 8 to 10 normal chromosomes); reading the other parent off
sibling embryos (needs the other parent's array to tell siblings from strangers).

PASS TESTS (they add to the item's EXIT gate)
--------------------------------------------
D1. The EXIT gate: 37 of 60 and 92 of 158 in each one-parent arm, 0 wrong.
D2. No built gain is printed as a lost parent, in any arm.
D3. On the 270 with-parent rows and the 330 no-parent rows: nothing 6.4.1 prints is lost, no stated
    loss becomes a gain, no new call on a stated-normal chromosome, and the both-parent and no-parent
    rows are identical to 6.4.1.
D4. 0 wrong on the two extra builds (the other parent's extra copy as both homologues, and as the
    same homologue twice).
D5. All checks pass. Suite: no line worse. The suite gains a measured-father arm for constructed
    gains.
D6. Released as its own version, after a browser run. HELD if D1 is not met.

PREDICTED BEFORE THE RUN
------------------------
D1 met on the 60 (measured father 44, rebuilt father 37), NOT met on the 158 (about 81 and 74
against 92). D2 met (the 3 + 3 lost-parent prints on built gains withdrawn). D3 met: on the 31 cells
the rule changes nothing, because no chromosome there carries both an intensity gain call and a
lost-parent state. D4 met offline (measured 42 and 45, rebuilt 35 and 39 on the two builds, 0 wrong).
The gate run is the second look at the 158.

For the record: two recipient chromosomes of the suite already read as gains with two parents before
anything is built on them; no rule can name the parent of the copy added there.

FAULT FOUND IN THE GATE RUN, AND THE CHANGE (written before the rerun)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The suite's metamorphic test (every other marker, rebuilt father) shows one new claim: an "allele
imbalance" on a chromosome intensity does not call gained (half_new_claims 1 -> 2). Cause: the build
used the rule-2 weighted share for the WHOLE gain test, so the share, and with it the imbalance
verdict, moved on chromosomes intensity does not call gained. That breaks rule 4 ("a chromosome
intensity does not call a gain: nothing changes"); the rule itself is unchanged.
Change: the gain test runs as before (unweighted, every chromosome) and a second pass with rules 1 and
2 replaces the verdict only on chromosomes the intensity scan calls gained. Nothing the parent of a
constructed gain is named by can move (every name needs the intensity call on that chromosome), so
the constructed-gain lines of the gate run stand and the 158 are not read again; the invariance test
and the 60 are rerun.
After the change: invariance identical to 6.4.0 on every line (half_new_claims back to 1), the 60
unchanged (rebuilt 37, measured 44, both 37), all checks pass.

Why D2 fails on 3: rule 1 acts only where the intensity scan calls the gain. A built gain that
intensity misses and whose copy-state share crosses into a loss state is still printed as a lost
parent. The same thing shows once on the 60's both-homologue build. Telling such a gain from a lost
copy without intensity needs the calls (opposite homozygotes, heterozygous calls), which this rule
does not use.
