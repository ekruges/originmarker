Finding the extra copies: the rule, the gate, and what it scored
================================================================
Written 18 November 2026 BEFORE the rule below was built or run, as plan rule 8 requires. Plan items
3.1, 3.2 and 3.3. Open arrays only: every list row is checked against audit/local/truth/units.tsv
before a file is opened, and a run holding a sealed or exposed array is refused. The 123 sealed
arrays were not read. Cells are given by number or by the array id of a published series; no
laboratory barcode is in this file.

WHAT IS WRONG TODAY, with the cause of each
-------------------------------------------
Measured on 6.9.0 (audit/local/gains-breaks-2026-11-17/report.txt) and on 6.4.1
(audit/local/lab-2026-11-16/report.txt). Nothing below is new work; it is the lead this rule follows.

1. GAINS ARE MISSED. Of the 53 stated gains on the 30 open cells the gain figure study scores
   (audit/truth/gain-figure-levers-2026-11-17.txt), 13 are called. Causes, per event:
     - 28 under the bar. The whole-chromosome arm asks |shift| >= 0.40 log2 and the piece arm
       >= 0.585. Real third copies in this material read 0.23 to 0.34 whole and 0.23 to 0.50 on a
       piece, so the entire observed population of real third copies sits under the piece bar and
       half of it under the whole-chromosome bar. 9 pieces whose region test already clears (797 to
       5056 against a bar of 250) are stopped by that bar alone.
     - 8 have no intensity reference.
     - 3 print as something else.
     - 24 of the 28 are in two cells whose gained chromosomes ARE the cell's majority, where no test
       read against the cell's own level can see them. Those are not reachable here and are not
       counted as reachable.
2. FALSE GAINS ON THE QUIET ARRAYS. 9 on 2 of the 72. Eight are whole chromosomes on one array at
   about twice the majority's intensity, with their markers AT the homozygous poles: one homologue,
   not three copies. The heterozygous-call veto (GAIN_HET_RATIO_MAX) refuses a raised group only
   where it carries MORE heterozygous calls than the majority, and this group carries 0.42x, so the
   veto reads one homologue as confirming a third copy. The ninth is a 1.7 Mb piece in the HLA
   region at 9.4x mean probe density.
3. A BUILT GAIN PRINTS AS A LOSS. 3 of 158 held-out constructions with the measured father
   (audit/truth/extra-copy-one-parent-2026-11-16.txt, gate D2). Cause, traced there: an extra copy
   of the loaded parent puts the copy-state share r at 2/3 of the cell's disomic level and an extra
   copy of the other parent's at 4/3, which on some cells crosses OTHER_LOST_MAX or PARENT_LOST_MIN.
   The guard against that (oneParentGain rule 1) acts only where the intensity scan CALLS a gain, so
   a gain the intensity sees but cannot call still prints as a lost parent.

THE RULE
--------
An extra copy is printed only on this evidence, in this order. 1 and 2 are both required; 3 only
says whose copy it is and never makes the call.

1. THE STRETCH STILL CARRIES TWO DIFFERENT ALLELES. P is the share of a stretch's markers whose B
   allele fraction lies between 0.2 and 0.8, which is the share sitting OFF the two homozygous
   poles. A raised whole chromosome or a nominated gain piece is printed only where

       P(stretch) >= 0.5 x median P(the chromosomes the intensity reference is taken among)

   and is otherwise withheld with its cause. WHERE THE BAR COMES FROM: a third copy of a normal
   chromosome carries both parents' homologues, so it keeps every marker that was off the poles
   (expected ratio about 1); one homologue doubled keeps none (about 0). 0.5 is the midpoint, and it
   was set before any score (audit/local/lab-2026-11-16/report.txt section 1, written 16 November;
   a bar of 0.3 was also looked at there and is NOT used, because its score was seen first).
   READABLE ONLY WITH A REFERENCE: fewer than 200 markers with a B allele frequency in the stretch,
   or fewer than 4 reference chromosomes, and the test cannot be read.
   CORRECTED WHILE BUILDING, before any array was run: this said the gain is then WITHHELD. Building
   it against a fixture whose B-allele frequencies all sit at the poles showed what that does: an
   array whose B allele frequency is clamped to its endpoints, which is how a cluster-file BAF is
   defined, has no off-pole markers anywhere, so every gain on it would be refused for a file format
   rather than for anything measured. The test is a veto, not the evidence the call rests on, so
   where it cannot be read it does not act and the limits say so. 25 of the 218 rows of the gain
   list have fewer than 4 reference chromosomes; on those the piece arm's test does not act either,
   by the same minimum.
   The existing veto is untouched and still fires on a raised GROUP carrying more heterozygous calls
   than the majority. This is its missing other end, per chromosome rather than per group.

2. THE INTENSITY IS UP BY AT LEAST A THIRD COPY'S WORTH ON THIS MATERIAL: 0.30 log2 against the
   cell's own level, with every relative test unchanged (a whole chromosome still needs z >= 5.49
   against the spread of the array's own chromosomes; a piece still needs its region test at 250
   and the 1 Mb span).
   WHOLE CHROMOSOMES ONLY, as variant B below. The piece arm was run once at this floor and is not
   given it: it printed 21 gains on 12 of the 72 arrays stated free of variation against 9 on 2
   today, in the dense-probe windows of the HLA region, chromosome 19 and 17p13. A piece arm needs
   a second channel rather than a smaller number; the one measured to work is the allele side.
   WHERE 0.30 COMES FROM: measured on the whole-chromosome arm, 17 November (plan 3.3): a floor of
   0.30 recovers 2 stated gains and adds 0 calls on 1,656 chromosomes published normal and 0 on the
   1,188 chromosomes of the 72 quiet arrays; 0.25 adds 1 call on a published-normal chromosome and
   0.20 adds 3. It is the lowest level the existing measurement shows to cost nothing, and the
   requirement it answers is this item's own gate line ("stated normal chromosomes left alone does
   not fall"). Why it is under 0.40: a third copy is log2(3/2) = 0.585 against a lost copy's 1.0, so
   one absolute floor for both directions asks 1.71x as much of a gain as of a loss.
   THE LOSS FLOORS ARE NOT TOUCHED: 0.40 whole, 1.0 on a piece, 0.14 on unamplified material. The
   bulk floor is not scaled either: 0.14 already sits under every bulk trisomy measured (0.299 to
   0.357), and nothing here measures bulk.
   THE OTHER PIECE ARM IS NOT TOUCHED. A piece nominated because the array STOPPED CALLING there
   and whose intensity went up (scanCopyNumber's gain arm) keeps the 0.585 bar: its region test is
   on the no-call rate, so the magnitude test is the only thing standing between it and a region the
   array merely failed on. The arm this rule lowers (scanCopyGain) nominates on the intensity
   itself, so its region test and its magnitude test read the same quantity.

3. WHOSE COPY IT IS. Unchanged. With both parents, the father's share at opposite homozygotes; with
   one parent, his share at his homozygous markers, and the heterozygous-call route upward only.

WHAT WITHHOLDS A GAIN, and what is withheld rather than reversed
----------------------------------------------------------------
4. A LOST PARENT IS NOT PRINTED WHERE THE INTENSITY SAYS THERE IS MORE DNA. With one parent, a
   chromosome whose copy-state share reads a lost parent is not printed as one where its intensity
   sits at or above +0.30 log2 against the cell's own level, whether or not the relative test lets a
   gain be CALLED there. It is scored as carrying both parents and goes to the gain test, exactly as
   rule 1 of audit/truth/extra-copy-one-parent-2026-11-16.txt already does on a called gain, and the
   limits say why no parent is named. WHY THE WEAKER TRIGGER IS RIGHT: a lost copy cannot raise
   intensity at all, so evidence that is not enough to assert a gain is still enough to refuse its
   opposite. A tool that reports the opposite direction is worse than one that says nothing.
5. Nothing above changes a loss call, a loss coordinate or a named lost parent anywhere the
   intensity does not read above the cell's own level by a third copy's worth.

JOY'S TWO CANDIDATES, measured here and kept only if they earn their place
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
C1. A GAIN READ FROM A SISTER CELL'S LOSS (plan 3.1, ask 4). The module and its bars exist and are
    not wired (audit/local/pending-3.1/sisterGain.ts, 14 November: an interval a sister shows lost
    is read in this cell against its own reference chromosomes, called at shift >= 0.25 and z >= 3).
    THE SAME-GENOME RULE IS PART OF THE CANDIDATE, as plan 3.1 states it: a sister counts only
    where the two arrays read as one genome, opposite homozygotes at or under 0.02 of their shared
    calls. Without it the reading trusts the embryo label, and on the laboratory's own cells only
    129 of 431 pairs sharing a label are one genome; 29 of its 30 calls came from a "sister" that is
    not. KEPT only if, with the same-genome rule, it adds stated gains and adds no call on a
    chromosome published normal.
C2. MARKERS WHERE THE PARENT CARRIES TWO DIFFERENT ALLELES (plan 3.2, ask 3). At a marker where the
    loaded parent is heterozygous, a cell carrying TWO of that parent's homologues must carry both
    of his alleles and so must read heterozygous; a cell carrying one reads heterozygous only where
    the other parent differs. So the share of heterozygous calls at the parent's heterozygous
    markers, against the same cell's reference chromosomes, should RISE on an extra copy of that
    parent that came from his two different homologues, and not move on one that is a single
    homologue doubled. Measured here as a candidate detector and as a candidate namer. KEPT only if
    it separates on the open stated gains that have a parent.

NOT IN THIS RULE, and why
-------------------------
- A 3 MB FLOOR ON GAIN PIECES, which is what removes the ninth quiet-array false gain (the HLA
  piece). The loss scans use 3 Mb; the gain scan has only 1 Mb. That is a stated limit on what the
  tool will claim, so it is the owner's to declare (plan ledger line 17) and it is not built here.
- READING PIECES AGAINST THE 8 OR MORE TWO-COPY CHROMOSOMES where the normal-majority gate has
  removed the intensity reference, which is the measured candidate for the parity defect ("a gain
  found with no parent is lost in every parent setup"). Measured on the 36 refused rows it adds 11
  losses that agree with the paper. G5 forbids changing a loss call here, so it is not built in this
  item; the defect stays open with its cause recorded.
- THE 2 PIECES WHERE BOTH BARS ALREADY CLEAR AND NO PIECE IS FORMED (z10:1 and z13:25, both 16q).
  Not traced on 17 November and not traced here.
- A STATED 16Q DUPLICATION whose intensity reads 0.62 BELOW that cell's own level over the stated
  stretch. The figure study measured it; the tool prints a copy loss there. It is not a missed gain
  and is not counted as one in G1.

TWO VARIANTS OF RULE 2's PIECE BAR, declared before the valid run
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The first run of rule 2 was thrown away: adding the off-pole count to the B-allele tally moved the
mis-clustering share's slot in the same tuple, so every chromosome read a false extreme share and a
loss moved on 13 rows. The fault was mine, in the harness sense, not the rule's; it is fixed, both
shares are now pinned by a check, and nothing was scored off that run. What it did show, before it
was thrown away, is that the piece arm at 0.30 prints gain pieces on the quiet arrays, which is the
cost this item's own gate forbids and which has never been measured. So the piece bar is run as two
variants, both written here before either is scored, and the one that meets the gate is kept:

  A  one gain floor for both arms, 0.30, as rule 2 above states it.
  B  the whole-chromosome arm at 0.30 and the intensity-nominated piece arm left at 0.585.

Whichever loses is recorded with its measured cost and is not tried again at a third figure: a bar
chosen by its score is what rule 8 forbids. If both fail a gate line, the item stops and the owner
decides.

C1, THE SISTER CELL'S LOSS, MEASURED (no tool code; the module of 14 November, unwired)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Readings: every interval a cell of the same embryo shows lost, read in this cell against its own
reference chromosomes, on the 257 open cells of 72 embryos the run records cover, on chromosomes the
cell does not already call. The same intervals read in cells of OTHER embryos are the null.
At the module's own bars (shift >= 0.25 and z >= 3):

  readings      sister 2,095, other embryos 77,480
  sister arm    called on 21 readings of stated GAINS (9 distinct cell-and-chromosome events),
                0 of 70 readings on chromosomes published NORMAL,
                0 of 17 on stated segmental losses, 0 of 2 on stated whole losses,
                91 on chromosomes with no stated answer
  null arm      47 of 12,131 readings on published-normal chromosomes (0.4%), and 1,265 of 58,446
                with no stated answer. The null is not clean on the gain classes: these embryos are
                one laboratory's chromosome-16 cutting experiments, so cells of DIFFERENT embryos
                share the same target region, and a between-embryo reading there is not a true null.

THE SAME-GENOME RULE, measured on the 18 arrays carrying those 9 events (153 pairs):
  within one published embryo   27 pairs, opposite homozygotes 0.0072 to 0.1160 of shared calls
  between embryos              126 pairs, 0.0330 to 0.1177
  At the rule's bar of 0.02, 7 of the 27 within-embryo pairs read as one genome and no
  between-embryo pair does.
  So the bar admits no stranger here, and it rejects most of the publication's own embryo-mates.
  The bar was set on the laboratory's arrays, where same-genome groups read 0.000 to 0.012 and the
  closest different pair 0.027. It does not transfer to these cells, whose within-embryo pairs
  overlap the between-embryo range almost entirely. That is a finding about the bar, not a reason
  to move it.

WHAT C1 WOULD ADD, with the same-genome rule applied: 4 of the 9 events survive, every one a stated
gain the tool misses today (a 9q piece and three chromosome-16 pieces, each from a sister whose
pair rate is 0.0072 to 0.0194). The other 5 are rejected because the publication's own embryo-mates
do not read as one genome (0.0202 to 0.1160). No stated-normal chromosome is called in either case.

SO C1 EARNS ITS PLACE AND IS NOT WIRED HERE. What wiring it needs, and why it is a build of its own:
the page keeps a run's other arrays as genotype calls only, one byte per probe, with no intensity,
so what a sister shows lost cannot be read from them as the tool reads it on the cell in hand. Seven
of the nine events come from a sister's WHOLE-chromosome loss, which a collapsed call rate shows
from calls alone, so that is the shape to build; the segment case needs the sister's intensity.
One more caution, measured: on the cell whose intensity reference the tool refuses, the harness fell
back to all 22 autosomes as the reference, and that is where one of the 9 events comes from. A wired
version must use the tool's own reference or refuse, as every other reading here does.
