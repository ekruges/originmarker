A part-lost chromosome is called lost as a whole
================================================
Written 17 November 2026 BEFORE the code below is built or run. Open arrays only. Plan item 3.4a.
The trace behind it is recorded outside the repository (per-array tables stay under audit/local/);
this file carries the rule, the pass tests, the prediction and, after the run, the scored result.

WHAT IS WRONG
-------------
The copy scans in scoreSample.ts do not look at a chromosome the intensity test already called
whole:
    const wholeByIntensity = new Set(result.chroms.filter((c) => c.aneuploidy).map((c) => c.chrom))
    const scannedCopyRaw = [...cnByChrom].filter(([c]) => !wholeByIntensity.has(c))
The comment above `whole` gives the reason: "a chromosome already called whole is not scanned for a
segment inside it, since that is the same event described twice." That holds while the whole test
fires only on whole events. Since 6.0.0 it fires more often: the whole-chromosome null is taken
inside the largest gap-connected group of chromosome medians (intensityNull.majorityScale), which
tightened the spread on chaotic cells, so the same median shift reads a much larger z (on the cells
of the trace: -2.6 to -9.4, -4.6 to -15.5, -1.0 to -16.6, -0.67 to -11.1). A deep loss on a third of
a chromosome now pulls the chromosome's median over both whole-chromosome gates, the chromosome is
called lost whole, and the piece, whose edge sits at a published cut, is never looked for.
On the 195 published break coordinates this took 7 edges within 5 Mb that 5.54.0 printed (two
blastomeres of one published embryo).

THE RULE
--------
Every test below is one the tool already has, with its existing constant.
1. The copy-number scan (scanCopyNumber, the no-call channel) also runs on an autosome the intensity
   test called LOST whole, with the same reference and bars. Only its copy-loss pieces are kept
   there. Chromosomes called gained whole are not scanned, as today.
2. The rest of that chromosome is its markers outside those loss pieces. It is put to the
   whole-chromosome test exactly as parentage.ts puts a chromosome to it: the median log R of the
   rest, against the same null (lrrNull: centre, scale, units), is a change when
   intensityDetects(calibratedZ(median, lrrNull), true) (z beyond Z_CHROMOSOME 5.49) AND
   |median - centre| >= the floor the array earned (COPY_SHIFT_FLOOR 0.40, or COPY_SHIFT_FLOOR_BULK
   0.14 on unamplified bulk); OR the rest's call rate over the genome's median chromosome call rate
   is under CALL_COLLAPSE 0.60. The rest needs at least 200 markers, the minimum the whole test
   asks of a chromosome.
3. Where loss pieces were found and the rest is NOT a change by (2), the pieces account for the
   chromosome's shift: the pieces are reported and the whole-chromosome loss is not (its
   `aneuploidy` and `aneuploidyParent` are cleared).
4. Where the rest IS a change by (2), or has fewer than 200 markers, the whole-chromosome loss
   stands. A loss piece is reported beside it only where its own median sits at least
   SEGMENT_LRR_SHIFT (1.0, the depth the scan asks of any loss piece below its baseline) below the
   rest's median: a further copy lost inside a chromosome already down one, which is a second event
   with its own edges. Other pieces there are the whole loss described twice and are dropped, as
   today.
5. Nothing else changes. The allele claims, the sets the likelihood scans and the homozygosity
   filter read (`whole`, `lost`, taken before the scan), the gain scan, every withholding gate (a
   withheld array records these pieces as withheld, like any other piece), the 80% restatement
   filter against allele-named losses: all as in 6.10.0.

PASS TESTS (plan item 3.4a, written there before this file)
  B1. The 7 published break coordinates lost since 5.54.0 have an edge within 5 Mb again.
  B2. Stated whole-chromosome losses found: not lower than 6.10.0 in any setup. No stated whole
      loss is turned into a piece.
  B3. Stated-normal chromosomes left alone: not lower than 6.10.0 in any setup.
  B4. Every chromosome whose call changes from whole to piece is listed with the stated answer
      beside it, and the published coordinate against the printed edge interval.
  B5. All checks pass; suite no worse; browser run; released as its own version.
  Counting: score.py's per-chromosome rule (a stated whole loss is found when a loss is printed on
  that chromosome, whole or piece; a stated normal is left alone when nothing is printed on it) and,
  for B1, the 6.0.0 break definition (nearest raw window edge, printed or withheld, within 5 Mb).

PREDICTION FROM THE RECORDS (before the run)
  B1. Turning the skip off on the four trace arrays brought back the same 5.54.0 pieces, at 0.9 to
      3.4 Mb from the 7 coordinates, deltas -1.8 to -2.1 against the reference, on chromosomes whose
      whole median reads -0.42 to -0.72. The records do not hold the rest's median. Estimated from
      the share each piece covers (16% to 36% of the chromosome): two to four of the five
      chromosomes have a rest under the 0.40 floor and convert by rule 3; one or two (the
      chromosome with a 31 Mb piece and a whole median of -0.69 most likely) keep the whole call and
      report the piece by rule 4, since the piece sits more than 1.0 below a rest near -0.6.
      Prediction: 7 of 7 by the B1 count either way. Certain only after the run.
  B2. A whole-loss call becomes a piece only where the scan finds a piece at least 1.0 below the
      reference inside it. Most stated whole losses are monosomies reading -0.4 to -0.7 with no such
      piece, and nullisomies are covered end to end by their pieces (rest under 200 markers or
      itself lost). Prediction: 0 stated whole losses become pieces; the count of found stated whole
      losses cannot fall, since a converted chromosome still prints a loss.
  B3. The rule touches only chromosomes already called lost whole and only replaces or adds loss
      claims there. A stated-normal chromosome with no whole call is untouched. Prediction: no
      change in any setup (certain, short of a fault in the build).
  B4. Expected: the five trace chromosomes, plus a few on chaotic arrays where a deep piece sits in
      a monosomy (they would show under rule 4, not as conversions).

FIRST RUN, AND THE FAULT IT FOUND (written before any rerun)
------------------------------------------------------------
Run once on tesla, 636 open rows (270 with a parent, 330 with none, 36 more with none that carry
published break coordinates or hand calls), against the 6.10.0 records of the same rows.
  B1 met: all 7 coordinates have an edge within 5 Mb again (0.88 to 3.43 Mb); 30 of 195 in all,
     against 12.
  B3 met: stated normals left alone identical in every setup.
  B2 FAILED on its second clause. Found counts are identical in every setup, but 4 stated whole
     losses were turned into pieces (one of them in all four setups): 7 chromosome-rows.
Cause, measured on those chromosomes with the build plus a debug line (no parent):
  1. The rest test reads "no change" where the rest is a full copy down but noisy. A monosomy with
     a deeper stretch on its distal 30%: rest shift -0.594, past the 0.40 floor, z -4.17 under 5.49.
     Rule 2 asked the rest to fail the whole-chromosome test, which needs BOTH the z and the floor
     to call a change; a noisy cell's rest fails the z and was then read as "at the cell's level".
     Failing to be significant is not reading at the level.
  2. Pieces covering nearly the whole chromosome leave a sliver near a telomere (543 to 1,129
     markers, 1 to 3% of the chromosome), and the sliver was taken as "the rest". On chr8 of two
     cells the sliver read -0.95 at z -2.93, so the whole call was dropped for a piece from 0.16 to
     144.4 Mb. The same flaw printed a whole-length piece BESIDE a whole call by rule 4 (chr7 of one
     cell, 0.04 to 146.19 Mb): the whole loss described twice.
Change (existing constants only; the bars do not move):
  2'. The rest reads at the cell's own level when |median - centre| is under the floor the array
      earned (COPY_SHIFT_FLOOR 0.40, or COPY_SHIFT_FLOOR_BULK 0.14) and its call rate over the
      genome median is not under CALL_COLLAPSE 0.60. The z is not asked: it tests whether a change
      is bigger than the array's noise, not whether there is none.
  4'. Where the loss pieces together cover 80% or more of the chromosome's span (first to last
      marker), they are the whole loss: the whole call stands and no piece is reported there. 80% is
      the restatement share the copy scan already uses against an allele-named loss.
Every other line of the rule is unchanged. Rerun on the same 636 rows, scored the same way.

DRAFT CHANGELOG PARAGRAPH
-------------------------
**A chromosome lost in part is no longer called lost whole.** A deep loss over a third of a
chromosome can move the chromosome's median past both whole-chromosome gates, and the tool then
called the whole chromosome lost and never looked for the piece or the edge where it was cut. A
chromosome called lost whole is now read again in pieces (`partLost.ts`): where the rest of it reads
at the cell's own level (shift under the floor the array earned, calls not collapsed) the pieces are
reported instead of the whole call; where the rest is a copy down as well the whole call stands,
with any piece a further copy down printed beside it; pieces over 80% of the chromosome are the whole
loss. No constant is new. On the open rows, with no parent, an edge lies within 5 Mb of 29 of the 195
published break coordinates (was 12), including the 7 lost since 5.54.0; stated whole losses found
and stated normals left alone are unchanged in every setup, and no stated whole loss becomes a piece.
