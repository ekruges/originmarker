The parent check against a reconstructed parent
===============================================
Scored on 15 November 2026 on open arrays only. Follows audit/truth/v6-2026-11-15.txt, whose
section 9 lists this as not done.

THE RULE, written before it was built or run in the tool
--------------------------------------------------------
  1. Markers: the adult is homozygous for an allele the founders hold at 0.02 to 0.2. An allele no
     founder holds is left out.
  2. The enrichment E is read only where at least 20% of the sample's calls at those markers are
     heterozygous.
  3. The bars are unchanged and the same for a measured and a reconstructed parent: three
     chromosomes present by D, or E at 5 or over, or the whole-genome clause.

Pass test: against the rebuilt father the 31 Table S3 cells keep the stage, zygosity, printed calls
and named parents they have in 6.0.0; no unrelated array carries a rebuilt father's genome;
measured-parent results unchanged; the suite no worse on any line.

WHY THOSE TWO CHANGES
---------------------
825 open arrays against two fathers, each as a measured array and as a rebuild from his pronuclei
(carry.ts). With every rare allele counted and the 6.0.0 rule:

  D reads a rebuild as it reads a measured array for true children (71 to 76 of 77 by three
  chromosomes, measured 75) and admits three noisy unrelated arrays against one rebuild and none
  against the measured array. E runs higher against a rebuild: unrelated arrays reach 6.4 against
  3.8 measured.

Both come from alleles no founder holds. A rebuild is made from amplified cells, and a homozygote for
such an allele in it is usually a miscall that other amplified arrays repeat; left out, the three
admissions go and E reads the same against both (unrelated arrays reach 3.8 against a rebuild, 3.3
measured). The unrelated arrays that read E over 5 against a rebuild were near-homozygous cells,
3% heterozygous, whose few heterozygous calls are noise in both classes; a ratio of two noise rates
is not a reading, which is the second change.
