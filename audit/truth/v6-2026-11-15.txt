2. WHOSE GENOME IS THIS
-----------------------
Two readings at markers where the adult is homozygous for an allele the founder panel holds at 0.2
or under.

  D = (sample the same homozygote - sample the other homozygote) / called. A child reads above
      zero at any allele dropout, because dropout makes the two kinds of false homozygote in equal
      numbers; anyone else reads below zero; the adult's own haploid product reads near one.
  E = the share of calls that are heterozygous there, over the same share where the adult is the
      common homozygote. A heterozygote needs both alleles, so no dropout makes one. A child reads
      a median of 8.7; an unrelated genome 1.5 and never over 3.8.

A chromosome is `present` or `absent` at 2.5 standard errors of D, and not read where the control
class shows its calls are noise. The array carries the genome on three chromosomes present, or on E
at 5 or over, or where D pooled over ten or more chromosomes is 2.5 standard errors above zero and
no chromosome is absent. It carries none where no chromosome is present and ten are absent.

6. THE INTENSITY NULL AND THE GAINS
-----------------------------------
`nullScale` is the median and scaled median absolute deviation of the 22 autosome medians, and
holds while three or four are displaced. `majorityScale` splits the sorted medians wherever two
neighbours stand more than 0.20 apart and takes the null inside the largest group. An array with
no such gap is one group and reads exactly as before. A chromosome two thirds heterozygous has no
template and is not a copy state the others are measured against.

A group of four or more chromosomes above the majority is reported as gained only if it carries
at most 0.9 of the majority's heterozygous calls. One step up is a third copy over two, or two
copies over a majority at one, and intensity cannot say which. Nine chromosomes whose sisters lack
exactly those nine read 0.79; seven stated-normal chromosomes above twelve stated losses read 1.22.

What the stated gains are, on the arrays that have them:

  Z10-4, Z10-5   Twelve "trisomies" each. Nine chromosomes are absent from each array (two thirds
                 heterozygous, 1.3 log2 down), so the twelve present sit high against the array's
                 median. v6 reads those losses and one gain, chromosome 6, in each.
  Z10-6          Nine stated trisomies. They are exactly the nine chromosomes Z10-4 and Z10-5 lack,
                 and Z10-6 lacks chromosome 6. v6 reads eight of the nine, and the loss of 6.
  Z17-51, Z15-38 Nine and four stated trisomies that are the upper end of a continuous spread of
                 chromosome medians, with stated-normal chromosomes within 0.06 of them. Not read.

Gains and sister cells. Of the whole-chromosome gains printed on open cells that have a sister on
the array, 32 of 52 are met by a whole-chromosome loss of the same chromosome in a sister. For the
5.54.0 code it is 8 of 26.

9. WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO
------------------------
  The parent check does not yet read a REBUILT parent, which is the laboratory's usual
  configuration. Measured since: against a father rebuilt from his pronuclei, D reads true children
  as it does against his measured array (71 to 76 of 77, measured 75) and reads no unrelated array
  as his (0 of 159, 0 of 323). E runs higher against a rebuild, unrelated arrays reaching 6.4
  against 3.8, because about a quarter of a rebuild's rare homozygotes are the father's
  heterozygotes. So D can be read against a rebuild as it stands and E needs its own bar.

  A full sibling, or the sample's own child, carries the genome too. Which of two arrays is the
  parent is not in the genotypes (relatedness.ts).

  How many genomes an array holds when it carries none of the adult's, or when no parent is loaded.
  Parent-free heterozygosity does not count genomes on this material: pronuclei read 5% to 26%
  heterozygous and diploid blastomeres 10% to 35%. Of seven pronuclei the parent-free stage reads
  two haploid.

  Gains on a segment, with no parent, on a drop-in array: withheld.

  One parent against two: segment losses 13 of 20 against 18 of 20; the parent of a constructed
  extra chromosome is named on fewer cells with one parent than with two (section 7).
