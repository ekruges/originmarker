REPLACING THE SEGMENT NULL: THE DISTRIBUTION FIRST, THEN THE ESTIMATOR, THEN ONE READ

Committed before any of it is measured. The order matters and is the point of this file: the
estimator is derived from the shape of the distribution rather than chosen from a list, and the
choice is then scored once against a bar fixed here.

WHAT IS WRONG WITH THE NULL THAT SHIPS

`externalNull` is the MEDIAN per-chromosome absence rate over the sample's other autosomes. 5.43.0
established what that costs: a median holds while most autosomes are unaffected, 8 of the 31 public
cells carry 11 or more abnormal of 22, and past that the bar rises until a true event cannot clear
the calling threshold. Measured, over the minimum window, a true single-parent loss scores 958 at
the null floor of 0.002 and 226 at a null of 0.028 against a threshold of 250. Two of the three
cells whose hand-called terminal deletion the scan misses sit at 0.0280 and 0.0282.

An external review declined to propose a replacement and gave the reason: on a cell with 15 of 22
affected, no robust estimate of LOCATION over the sample's own chromosomes can work, so the
replacement may have to stop referencing the sample. That is the open question here.

STEP 1. THE SHAPE, MEASURED BEFORE ANY ESTIMATOR IS NAMED

For every one of the 31 cells, both absence runs, every autosome: the per-chromosome absence rate,
the informative marker count, and the hand karyotype's reading of that chromosome.

  T1. The per-chromosome rates are BIMODAL on a cell carrying events, with a lower mode near the
      floor on chromosomes the hand reads as intact and an upper mode near 0.115 to 0.124 on those
      it reads as lost. That figure is the measured rate a true single-parent loss produces,
      0.1244 against the mother and 0.1153 against the father, computed from the parental
      genotypes alone.
  T2. On the cells where the median fails, the chromosomes the hand reads as normal still sit at
      the lower mode. If they do, a low quantile recovers the null and the problem is the choice of
      estimator. If they do NOT, the whole genome's absence rate is raised and no within-sample
      estimator can work, which is the review's suspicion and would decide this.
  T3. The count of autosomes the hand reads as normal, per cell, is reported beside the rates. A
      within-sample null needs some; GSM4472409 at 22 of 22 abnormal and GSM4552418 at 21 of 22
      have none, and for those the honest answer is the blindness that 5.43.0 already reports.

  FALSIFIED IF T1 fails: if the rates are unimodal or the upper mode is not near the measured event
  rate, then the model of what a lost chromosome looks like is wrong, and nothing below should be
  attempted until that is understood.

STEP 2. THE ESTIMATOR, DERIVED FROM STEP 1 AND NOT BEFORE

No candidate is named here, deliberately. What Step 1 shows about the shape determines the form:
a bimodal distribution with a populated lower mode calls for a low-quantile or lower-mode
estimator, and the quantile follows from how many autosomes are affected at worst rather than from
a search. A distribution with no clean mode calls for a parent-referenced null, whose value is an
error rate rather than a sample statistic. Writing the form down before looking is what produced
the median.

STEP 3. ONE READ, AGAINST A BAR FIXED NOW

The truth is the authors' by-eye karyotype over the 595 autosomes of the 31 cells with both parents
arrayed. Scored for the shipped median null and for whatever Step 2 derives:

  hand-called losses detected
  chromosomes the hand reads as normal that get a call
  chromosomes where `canDetectLoss` says the scan is blind, which is the coverage endpoint

THE OBJECTIVE, AND IT IS NOT LEXICOGRAPHIC. The segment statistic pre-registration in this
directory used "maximise detections subject to a cap" and recorded that the rule walked to the
corner of its grid, because a lexicographic rule sits on its constraint whenever the constraint is
slack. One number instead:

  detections  -  k x (calls on hand-normal chromosomes)

with k = 2.0, committed here. The reason for that value and not 1: this tool's output is which
parent lost a copy, on material heading toward clinical work, and a region named with a parent that
is not there is acted on while a missed region is silent. An external review worked the same
exchange rate over an earlier grid and found the breakeven between a conservative and an aggressive
operating point at k = 1.25, so a k of 1 is not conservative enough to avoid the corner and 2.0 is
inside the range where the conservative point wins on its own merits.

COVERAGE IS REPORTED AS A THIRD ENDPOINT and not folded into the objective. Two operating points
that scan different numbers of chromosomes are not comparable on raw detection counts, which is a
defect the same review found in the earlier grid: coverage there ran from 1.84 to 99.49 percent
across the settings being compared. Here both nulls scan the same chromosomes, so coverage is a
check that they do rather than a term to be priced.

THE BAR. The replacement ships only if it scores higher on the objective AND does not reduce the
number of chromosomes scanned. Better on detections while calling more hand-normal chromosomes is
a trade, and this file is where a trade gets refused rather than argued: the last bar in this
directory was missed by one chromosome and the statistic did not ship.

WHAT IS NOT IN SCOPE

The survivor-share normalisation. An external review showed its own form, dividing by the count of
single-parent-allele markers rather than by all informative markers, separates the refused cells
where the shipped form does not, 0.59 to 0.92 against 0.22 to 0.52. It has no threshold calibrated
against that denominator and calibrating one is a separate pre-registration. Nothing here depends
on it.

THE RESULT, WRITTEN AFTER THE RUN

Nothing above this line was changed.

T1 FAILS in the half that decides this, and the file said that stops the work. It does.

  what the hand karyotype says            n     p10      median     p90
  this parent's copy LOST               131  0.0429      0.0987  0.1152
  the chromosome NORMAL                 954  0.0064      0.0210  0.0411
  a loss of the OTHER parent            215  0.0043      0.0135  0.1765

The upper mode is where it was predicted: a lost chromosome reads 0.0987 at the median against a
measured event rate of 0.1153, so the model of what a loss looks like holds.

The lower mode is not. A chromosome the hand reads as NORMAL reads 0.0210 at the median and 0.0411
at the ninetieth percentile, against a floor of 0.002. Ten times the floor. And the blindness
boundary established in 5.43.0 is a null of 0.0253, so an intact chromosome on this material sits
at the edge of it before any event is involved. The two distributions nearly touch: lost at the
tenth percentile is 0.0429 and normal at the ninetieth is 0.0411.

T2 FAILS, and it fails in a way no estimator can repair.

`normOnly` below is the median taken over ONLY the chromosomes the hand karyotype reads as normal:
the best any within-sample null could do if it knew which they were.

  cell         norm  abn   median   normOnly   blind on the median?

  GSM4552389     20    2   0.0246     0.0244   BLIND
  GSM4472422     18    4   0.0245     0.0240   BLIND

Eight of the twenty cells with a usable median are blind, and look at the first four: twenty, twenty,
eighteen and nineteen autosomes read normal by hand. On those cells the median IS a clean
chromosome. The estimator is working correctly and the measurement is too noisy for it to matter.
On GSM4552374, the cleanest of them at 20 normal against 2 abnormal, the oracle null is 0.0287 and
the boundary is 0.0253: perfect knowledge of which chromosomes are intact still leaves it blind.

And on GSM4472405 and GSM4552420 the hand-normal chromosomes themselves read 0.14 and 0.08. There
is no clean population on those cells at all.

T3 as predicted: GSM4472409, GSM4552418 and GSM4552422 carry zero hand-normal autosomes, and all
three are correctly reported blind by 5.43.0.

SO NO NULL IS SHIPPED, AND NONE SHOULD BE. The estimator was never the binding constraint. The
contrast is: an intact chromosome reads 0.021 and a lost one reads 0.0987, a factor of 4.7, and
over the minimum window of 2,400 markers that scores 188 against a calling threshold of 250.
Replacing the median with any low quantile moves a number that was not what stopped the scan.

WHERE THE LIMIT ACTUALLY IS, AND WHAT IT POINTS AT

The diluted marker set cannot carry this. At the measured contrast a window needs about 4,800
markers to clear the threshold, which scores 375, and an event occupying 18 percent of a chromosome
does not contain 4,800 one-parent-homozygous markers; a 4,800-marker window placed over it would be
half normal sequence and its rate would fall back toward the null.

The opposite-homozygote set does carry it, and now the reason is arithmetic rather than a grid
search. On markers where both parents are homozygous for opposite alleles a biparental sample is
obligate heterozygous, so a lost copy reads absence near 1 rather than near 0.1:

  set                       markers in 16q23.1-tel   event rate   LRT   clears 250?
  one-parent-homozygous                      ~2,700       0.0987   188   no
  opposite-homozygote                           245       0.70+    514   yes

At an event rate of 0.80 against this null, 97 markers suffice. Counted directly, 16q23.1-tel
carries 245 of them.

This is the second independent route to the same place. `audit/segment-statistic-preregistration.txt`
tested the opposite-homozygote statistic against a pre-registered bar and it failed by one
chromosome, on a grid whose two most conservative window settings turned out to reach 1.84 and
36.62 percent of chromosomes. An external review then argued the window floor should be DERIVED
from contrast rather than searched. This measurement supplies the contrast, and the derived floor
for the undiluted set is around a hundred markers rather than 2,400.

So the next pre-registration is that statistic with a contrast-derived floor, scored against the
595-autosome truth, and not another estimator for a null that was not the problem.
