Gain recall splits by whether the array's homozygous BAF poles survived
=======================================================================

Run 8 November 2026 against 5.47.0 / 47e48d4. Harness audit/joy-pole-fraction.ts over the 21
hand-call arrays. Per-region table, which carries barcodes, is audit/local/joy-pole-fraction-
2026-11-08.tsv. Laboratory prefixes pseudonymised per audit/local/barcode-map.tsv.

THE QUESTION. The gains benchmark reports 11 of 26. Consult A observed that ten of those eleven
sit in cells carrying no hand-called loss at all, at log2R deviations of +1.61 to +2.83, and that
a third copy is log2(3/2) = 0.585. It predicted the recall was agreement on amplification-distorted
material rather than detection of a third copy, and gave a falsifiable test: the fraction of
markers at a homozygous BAF pole, min(BAF, 1 - BAF) < 0.1, inside each hand region. Prediction,
fixed before the run: found gains below 0.5, missed gains near 0.83.

MEASURED.

  found gains    n = 11    pole fraction median 0.340    range 0.176 to 0.844
  missed gains   n = 15    pole fraction median 0.791    range 0.202 to 0.855

The prediction holds. Split at 0.60:

  gains on pole-INTACT regions      2 of 14     14%
  gains on pole-COLLAPSED regions   9 of 12     75%

At array level the same split is sharper. Every cell whose genome-wide pole fraction is under 0.22
has near-complete gain recall, and every cell above 0.34 has almost none:

  genome pole fraction under 0.22    5 cells     10 of 12 gains found
  genome pole fraction over 0.34     4 cells      1 of 14 gains found

WHAT THIS MEANS. The headline 11 of 26 is carried by five arrays whose BAF poles have collapsed.
On arrays where the poles are intact, which is where a real third copy would be legible, the
detector finds 2 of 14. The gate it clears on the collapsed arrays is a distortion signature, not
a trisomy: SEGMENT_LRR_SHIFT is 1.0 and these regions read +1.61 to +2.83, while one extra of two
copies predicts +0.585.

So the gains gap is not mostly edge placement, which is what the 4 November slide says. Recall on
the material where a gain is readable is 2 of 14.

TWO EXCEPTIONS, both kept in the count above.
  - Two found gains are pole-intact, at 0.731 and 0.844. The detector is not blind on this
    material, only close to it.
  - Two missed gains are pole-collapsed, at 0.202 and 0.225. Both are in the most collapsed cell
    and both are small, 3 Mb and 15 Mb, so the marker floor explains them.

LIMITATION OF THE RATIO COLUMN. The per-region table also reports pole fraction inside the region
over the same array's genome-wide figure. That ratio is above 1 for every region, gains included,
because the genome-wide baseline is itself dragged down by the array's own collapsed regions. The
baseline that would make the ratio meaningful is disomic arms only. Read the absolute pole fraction
inside the region, not the ratio.

AN OBSERVATION, NOT A FINDING. All 67 hand-called losses read a pole fraction under 0.60, median
near 0.10. A clean hemizygous loss leaves one chromosome and should read nearly every marker
homozygous, so a pole fraction near 1.0. These regions carry a median log2R deviation near -1.4,
beyond the -1.0 one of two copies predicts, so the likelier reading is that intensity has fallen
far enough for the BAF estimate itself to become unstable. Not pursued here, and not quoted to
anyone until it is.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE. Whether regions-dieter.tsv is a hand reading at all. Consult B found
all 93 rows are an exact subset of the offline script's output, on a 1 Mb grid, reproducible by a
threshold filter. Until that is answered with Dr Egli, every recall figure here, corrected or not,
is the tool measured against a table of unknown provenance.
