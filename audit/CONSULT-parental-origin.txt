
PER-EVENT PARENTAL ORIGIN IN A SINGLE-CELL SNP-ARRAY SERIES
Assessment of constructions A-E, with a recommended method
Affymetrix Axiom UK Biobank, 825,657 markers, GRCh37; 884 arrays, 1,189 CNV regions in 192 arrays

===============================================================================
0. HEADLINE
===============================================================================

Obstacle 1 as stated is not a geometric limit. It is an artefact of recentring on
the median. The two loss hypotheses sit SYMMETRICALLY about the disomic
expectation, at -q/2 and +q/2, where q is the alternate-allele frequency at the
informative marker. Simulated at ADO 0.308, 400k markers:

  q      E[share | CN2]    loss of KNOWN copy   loss of UNKNOWN copy
  0.10   0.9500            0.8998  (-0.050)     1.0000  (+0.050)
  0.25   0.8751            0.7499  (-0.125)     1.0000  (+0.125)
  0.40   0.8003            0.6007  (-0.200)     1.0000  (+0.200)

Analytic: E[share|CN2] = 1 - q/2, loss-of-known = 1 - q, loss-of-unknown = 1.
Headroom is q/2 on EACH side. The measured +0.019 / -0.090 asymmetry is what
happens when the centre is set to the median rather than the mean: for every
q < 0.5 the majority of known-parent-hom markers sit at share 1.0, so the
MEDIAN IS the ceiling and the upward headroom is 0.000 by construction.

  q      fraction of markers at share 1.0   median   mean     headroom above median / above mean
  0.10   0.916                              1.000    0.9502   +0.0000 / +0.0498
  0.25   0.788                              1.000    0.8749   +0.0000 / +0.1251
  0.40   0.661                              1.000    0.7995   +0.0000 / +0.2005

That is the generator of the six one-directional results: a statistic whose
centre is a boundary point of its own support can only move one way. The fix is
not a better parent panel. It is (i) centre on the model expectation 1 - q/2, not
on an order statistic of a boundary-heavy distribution, and (ii) drop the share
scalar for a per-marker likelihood, which needs no centring at all.

Second result, independent of the first: ADO cannot manufacture a direction.
Allele dropout sends AB to AA or BB with equal probability, so E[share] is
invariant in ADO (0.8754 / 0.8748 / 0.8748 / 0.8754 at ADO 0.000 / 0.199 /
0.308 / 0.600, analytic 0.8750). Dropout inflates variance only. Any
one-directional statistic in this dataset is a centring or conditioning defect,
not a dropout effect.

===============================================================================
1. THE CONSTRUCTIONS
===============================================================================

A. SIBLING CELLS AS THE REFERENCE - SOUND, and it is the load-bearing idea.

Informative marker class: markers where the embryo is heterozygous, established
from event-FREE sibling cells of the same embryo, and where the region in the
affected cell has CN != 2 from the intensity channel. Heterozygosity of the
EMBRYO is what makes a marker one-way; it does not require either parent.

Critical detail: het must be established from SIBLING cells, never from the
affected cell's own genotype. Conditioning on the affected cell's own het call
forces share = 0.5 exactly (simulated mean 0.5000, zero markers at either tail),
destroys all information, and re-derives the region from heterozygosity, which
the brief forbids. Sibling-established het keeps both tails: 15.3% of markers at
1.0 and 15.3% at 0.0 in the same simulation.

phi is the fraction of the informative set that is FALSELY het. It is the only
quantity that biases this statistic one way, because a false het always retains
the reference allele and therefore always reads as loss-of-the-other-copy.
phi is driven by DROP-IN (false het at a homozygous marker), and this project has
already measured drop-in on 7 real 1C WGA arrays: 0.0569-0.0939, median 0.0854.
That is roughly 85-fold higher than a nominal 1e-3 genotyping error, and it
changes the design conclusion, so the rest of this section uses the measured
value.

THE FIXED ">=2 SIBLING CELLS" RULE IS UNSAFE, and it fails in the direction that
matters: at measured drop-in, phi INCREASES with the number of siblings, because
more cells mean more chances for a spurious het call to appear somewhere.

  clean sibs k   phi at fixed rule >=2   phi at scaled rule >=ceil(k/2)
  2              0.0512                  0.0512  (>=2)
  3              0.0864                  0.0864  (>=2)
  4              0.1317                  0.1317  (>=2)
  5              0.1835                  0.0229  (>=3)
  6              0.2375                  0.0378  (>=3)
  8              0.3393                  0.0105  (>=4)

So the het rule must SCALE with k, not sit at a constant 2. Use >=ceil(k/2) with
a floor of 2. Under the fixed rule, adding sibling arrays makes the bias worse
while appearing to make the het call more reliable, which is precisely the shape
of error that produced the six one-directional results.

There is a HARD CEILING on tolerable phi. Above roughly phi = 0.15-0.20 the
loss-of-reference arm becomes unresolvable at ANY marker count, because the
false-het floor exceeds the separation between hypotheses:

  phi     markers needed for the reference-copy-lost arm (eps 0.05 / eps 0.10)
  0.05    5    / 9
  0.10    10   / 13
  0.15    23   / 35
  0.20    unresolvable at n<=600, and not rescued by more markers
  >=0.25  unresolvable

This is a wall, not a slope. Any configuration whose phi exceeds ~0.15 must
refuse the call outright rather than report a low-confidence one. Note that the
fixed >=2 rule crosses that wall at k = 5 siblings.

Realistic operating points, blastomere ADO 0.308, drop-in 0.0854, eps 0.05:

  arrayed cells  clean sibs  rule   P(het est.)  phi      n_eff/Mb  min event
  3              2           >=2    0.479        0.0512   3.7       1,347 kb
  4              3           >=2    0.774        0.0864   6.0       1,667 kb
  5              4           >=2    0.910        0.1317   7.1       1,984 kb
  6              5           >=3    0.826        0.0229   6.4         781 kb
  8              7           >=4    0.863        0.0061   6.7         747 kb

Read the non-monotonicity carefully: from 3 to 5 arrayed cells the minimum
callable event gets WORSE (1,347 -> 1,984 kb) under the fixed rule, because
rising phi costs more than rising marker count gains. The scaled rule at 6-8
cells is where this route actually becomes good (about 750-780 kb).

Addressability. This route requires the event absent in >=2 arrayed sibling
cells, so with median 3 cells/embryo it addresses mitotic events in embryos with
>=3 arrayed cells, and at 3 cells the het-establishment rate is only 0.479.
It does NOT address meiotic events present in every cell. Given "an almost equal
number of meiotic and mitotic aneuploidies" in a comparable PGT-M series
(Kubikova/Handyside 2025, Sci Rep), plus the requirement of >=3 cells, expect
this route alone to reach well under half of the 1,189 regions. It is a
necessary component, not a complete answer.

B. RELATIVE (UNNAMED) ASSIGNMENT PLUS ONE ANCHOR PER FAMILY - SOUND, and it is
the correct answer to the power question.

Relative assignment is valid: sibling embryos are repeated draws from four
founder haplotypes, and same-haplotype vs different-haplotype is estimable
without naming a parent. It does cost real power, because only the unordered
split is observed and each family's direction must be inferred:

  configuration          p_true   power named   power unnamed
  10 donors x 5 events   0.60     0.412         0.106
  10 x 10                0.65     0.921         0.504
  20 x 5                 0.65     0.916         0.316
  20 x 10                0.65     0.997         0.716
  30 x 10                0.65     1.000         0.860

  minimum detectable paternal fraction at 80% power:
  10 x 5   named 0.676   unnamed 0.779
  20 x 5   named 0.626   unnamed 0.729
  20 x 10  named 0.588   unnamed 0.662
  30 x 10  named 0.572   unnamed 0.642

Unnamed assignment does not reach the 0.60 claimable floor at any configuration
tested. Named assignment reaches it at 20 donors x 10 events. So the anchor is
not a convenience, it is the difference between a claimable and an unclaimable
result.

The anchor economy is the strongest fact in this consult. In the 2PN groups
there is ONE egg donor per group. A single maternal anchor therefore names all
four founder haplotypes for EVERY embryo in that group. Arrays needed: one per
egg donor, not one per event-carrying cell. The 0-of-192 intersection does not
apply, because the anchor never has to be in the same array as the event.

Anchor options, in order of preference:
  1. Cumulus/granulosa cells - somatic, maternal, diploid, bulk-quality, and one
     experiment already includes a cumulus sample. This is the cheapest correct
     anchor available and it is already in the freezer.
  2. A PB1+PB2 pair from one oocyte of that donor. Per this project's prior
     computation, PB1+PB2 observe 3 chromatids of the {A,A,B,B} tetrad and prove
     maternal heterozygosity at 100% of maternal-het markers, so a PB pair is a
     complete maternal anchor. PB1 alone is not (p_SDS genome-wide 0.4357).
  3. A sperm array for the single sperm donor, same logic on the paternal side.

C. RECONSTRUCTED FATHER x POLAR-BODY MOTHER - UNSOUND AS SPECIFIED, salvageable
only in the MII-plus-sperm arm.

Three separate problems.
  (i) Availability. Father reconstruction needs 8+ embryos across 3+ oocyte
      donors. That is the MII-plus-sperm structure. The 2PN groups have one egg
      donor, so the construction is unavailable exactly where most of the
      material is.
  (ii) A single PB1 is not a maternal genotype. It is 2C and carries both sister
      chromatids of the NON-transmitted homologue, so it reports the homologue
      the oocyte did not receive. Used as "the mother" it produces the same
      class of error as the reconstructed mother: markers where she is actually
      het get admitted and read as maternal absence. That is the mechanism that
      turned -0.40 into +2.18, and PB1 alone will reproduce it.
  (iii) The father's missing 34.4% of markers IS directional, and predictably so.
      Reconstruction covers markers where the founder haplotypes are
      distinguishable in the embryo panel, i.e. it is enriched for high-MAF
      markers and depleted where the donor is homozygous. Since headroom is q/2,
      the retained set has systematically LARGER q and the missing set smaller
      q. Any comparison that pools the covered and uncovered fractions without
      weighting compares two different effective headrooms and will read as a
      parental asymmetry that is really a coverage asymmetry. If C is used at
      all, the obligate-het set must be restricted to markers covered on BOTH
      sides and the comparison stratified by q.

With a PB1+PB2 PAIR rather than a single PB1, and restricted to MII-plus-sperm
embryos, and stratified by q, C becomes sound. As written it is not.

D. INTENSITY PLUS GENOTYPE JOINTLY - SOUND AS AN INPUT, INSUFFICIENT AS A FIX.

The premise is right and worth keeping: dropout removes a genotype call without
removing DNA, so log2R and the genotype channel disagree on dropout and agree on
a real event. That is a genuine and useful discriminator, and it is how the
refusal state gets teeth.

But it does not rescue the one-parent case, because the one-parent problem was
never a dropout problem. Section 0 shows headroom is symmetric and ADO is
mean-unbiased; the asymmetry came from the centring. Adding an intensity channel
to a mis-centred share statistic yields a mis-centred joint statistic. D belongs
in the model as the CN clamp and as a dropout-vs-deletion discriminator, not as
the answer to obstacle 1.

E. COPY-NUMBER-AWARE INHERITANCE HMM OVER FOUR FOUNDER HAPLOTYPES - THIS IS THE
RECOMMENDED SHAPE. State space below.

===============================================================================
2. RECOMMENDED METHOD
===============================================================================

A per-embryo HMM over four latent founder haplotypes {A,B,C,D}, unnamed within
family, with copy number CLAMPED from the intensity channel, plus one anchor per
egg donor to convert unnamed to named.

State space. Emitting states are multisets of founders of size CN, with each
founder appearing at most twice (two sister chromatids):

  CN=0  nullisomy              1 state
  CN=1  monosomy/hemizygous    4 states   A B C D
  CN=2  disomy                10 states   AA AB AC AD BB BC BD CC CD DD
  CN=3  trisomy               16 states   AAB AAC AAD ABB ABC ABD ACC ACD ADD
                                          BBC BBD BCC BCD BDD CCD CDD
  CN=4  tetrasomy             19 states
  total                       50 emitting states

Plus one GLOBAL per-family latent nuisance variable: which unordered pair,
{A,B} or {C,D}, is maternal. One bit. The anchor observes it directly. Without
an anchor it is marginalised, which is precisely the named/unnamed power gap in
section 1B.

Transitions. Within a CN block: crossover only, per parent, with rates from a
SEX-SPECIFIC cM map, not a flat 1 cM/Mb - female autosomal crossover rate is
about 1.6-fold male (Bherer 2017), and the direction reverses near telomeres.
Between CN blocks: clamped, probability 1 at intensity-called breakpoints. The
regions are NOT re-derived from heterozygosity; the HMM is conditioned on them.

Emissions. Per marker, P(observed genotype | founder multiset) with an explicit
ADO parameter fitted per array, and a clustered-dropout correction. Dropout is
Markov with P(D|D) = 2.18 x r, giving lag-1 autocorrelation rho = a - b and
variance inflation (1+rho)/(1-rho):

  stage           ADO     P(D|D)   rho     VIF    n_eff
  blastomere      0.308   0.671    0.525   3.21   n/3.21
  single ESC      0.199   0.434    0.293   1.83   n/1.83
  trophectoderm   0.050   0.109    0.062   1.13   n/1.13
  bulk            0.013   0.028    0.016   1.03   n/1.03

Do not report an effective marker count without this divisor. A 200-marker
window in a blastomere carries the information of ~62 independent markers.

Informative marker density for the loss call. The informative class is
(reference hom) x (embryo het), whose expected fraction is exactly H/2 where H is
the diploid het rate - identity mean(pq) = H/2:

  H       informative markers   per Mb
  0.15    61,924                20.0
  0.22    90,822                29.3
  0.30    123,849               40.0

At H = 0.22, k = 3 siblings with a >=2 rule, and cell call rate 0.85: 19.3
usable markers/Mb, falling to 6.0 independent markers/Mb after VIF 3.21.

Per-event call and refusal rule. Three hypotheses per region: loss of the
reference-side copy, loss of the other copy, and uninformative/artefact
(share 0.5, i.e. the region is a dropout cluster, not a deletion). Take the
posterior over the three; CALL only if max posterior > 0.95, else REFUSE.
Markers needed, at genotyping error eps and false-het fraction phi:

  eps    phi     n needed, reference copy lost   other copy lost
  0.02   0.000   5                               5
  0.05   0.012   5                               5
  0.05   0.110   14                              5
  0.10   0.110   17                              9

Minimum callable event size, at MEASURED drop-in 0.0854 and the scaled het rule:
  6 arrayed cells (k=5, rule >=3): phi 0.023, n_eff 6.4/Mb  ->  about 780 kb
  8 arrayed cells (k=7, rule >=4): phi 0.006, n_eff 6.7/Mb  ->  about 750 kb
  4 arrayed cells (k=3, rule >=2): phi 0.086, n_eff 6.0/Mb  ->  about 1.7 Mb
  3 arrayed cells (k=2, rule >=2): phi 0.051, n_eff 3.7/Mb  ->  about 1.3 Mb
An idealised phi of 0.012 would give about 830 kb at 6.0 markers/Mb, but that phi
is not attainable at this platform's measured drop-in and should not be planned
against.

The stated breakpoint resolution is a median 9 markers / 60 kb. That is the
resolution of the BREAKPOINT, not the information content of the REGION. Events
smaller than ~1 Mb will be detected and refused a parental call. State this
plainly rather than reporting a call for them.

GAINS ARE A SEPARATE AND MUCH HARDER PROBLEM. With only one reference parent,
a CN3 region at reference-hom markers gives:
  maternal extra copy, mother AA           -> A,A,B  share 0.667
  paternal extra, father BB                -> A,B,B  share 0.333
  paternal extra, father AB                -> unresolved between the two
Separating 0.667 from 0.333 at 95% power needs >=117 informative markers, about
19.5 Mb at the usable density, and only WITH both parents. For the 318 gains,
the honest position is: whole-chromosome and large segmental gains are callable
in the anchored arm; sub-10-Mb gains are not. Note also that the embryo-only
gain-of-heterozygosity approach (Ranjbar 2026, RBMO) identifies MEIOTIC trisomy
without parents but does NOT assign a parent, and requires ~150 het markers per
bin with >=3 consecutive bins; it is a meiotic/mitotic classifier, not a
parental-origin method.

===============================================================================
3. WHAT NAMES THE PARENT WITHOUT AN ANCHOR
===============================================================================

One anchor-free discriminator survives scrutiny, and it is worth building as an
internal consistency check even though it should not be the primary evidence.
Female autosomal crossover count exceeds male by about 1.6-fold (Bherer 2017;
Halldorsson 2019 carries an erratum, PMID 30733390 - consult it). Once the
HMM has resolved haplotype groups, the group with more crossovers is maternal:

  meioses per group   P(correct call)
  1                   0.971
  2                   0.997
  3                   0.999
  5                   1.000

This assumes Poisson counts at mu_f = 42, mu_m = 26 per meiosis and, more
importantly, assumes crossovers are correctly counted. In WGA single cells at
ADO 0.308 the crossover count is itself noisy and biased toward false positives
from switch errors, which inflates BOTH groups and erodes the ratio. Treat the
tabulated probabilities as an upper bound and use this as corroboration of an
anchor-based naming, never as a substitute for one. If it disagrees with the
anchor, the haplotype resolution is wrong.

Note the check that keeps this honest: crossover-count naming and anchor naming
are independent, so their agreement rate is a direct empirical estimate of the
haplotype-resolution error rate.

===============================================================================
4. WHAT THE SIBLING-EMBRYO PANEL CANNOT DO ALONE
===============================================================================

Founder reconstruction from sibling embryos alone does not converge, which is
the quantitative reason an anchor is required rather than merely preferable.
Simulated per-locus identifiability, MAF 0.25:

  n embryos   ADO     P(>=1 het seen)   P(>=2 genotypes)   P(all 3 genotypes)
  3           0.308   0.506             0.532              0.080
  5           0.308   0.600             0.628              0.229
  8           0.308   0.662             0.673              0.375
  12          0.308   0.677             0.680              0.477
  12          0.050   0.679             0.642              0.203

Saturation at about 0.68 with 12 embryos is not a dropout limit - it barely
moves between ADO 0.308 and 0.050. It is the founder-configuration limit: at
roughly a third of loci the four founders do not differ enough to be
distinguished at all, whatever the sample size. This is consistent with the
reported 65.6% marker coverage of father reconstruction and is an independent
confirmation of that number.

===============================================================================
5. STATED FAILURE MODES
===============================================================================

1. phi, the false-het fraction, is the one-directional term. A falsely het
   marker always retains the reference allele, so it always reads as loss of the
   OTHER copy. At this platform's MEASURED drop-in (0.0854 median, 0.0939 max on
   7 real 1C WGA arrays) a FIXED >=2-sibling rule drives phi from 0.051 at k=2 to
   0.339 at k=8 - i.e. adding arrays makes the bias worse - and crosses the
   unresolvable wall (phi ~0.15-0.20) at k=5. Use >=ceil(k/2), floor 2. Report
   phi with every analysis; if phi is not reported the analysis is not
   interpretable, and if phi > 0.15 the call must be refused rather than
   downgraded.
2. Centring on any order statistic of a boundary-heavy distribution re-creates
   the original artefact. Centre on the model expectation 1 - q/2, or use the
   per-marker likelihood and do not centre at all.
3. Anchor misassignment inverts one family wholesale. A cumulus sample
   contaminated with, or swapped for, another donor's material flips every call
   in that group. Verify each anchor against its embryos by requiring
   biparental-consistent transmission at >=95% of markers, using the same
   stricter criterion that caught the 10 false children out of 13.
4. Coverage-dependent q stratification (obstacle in C) will read as parental
   asymmetry. Always stratify by q, or restrict to markers covered on both
   sides.
5. The refusal state must be reported as a count, not suppressed. If the number
   of events refused is not stated alongside the split, the split is a
   conditional statistic on an unstated selection.
6. Crossover-count naming inherits every haplotype-resolution error. Use as a
   check, never as the primary call.

===============================================================================
6. HONEST BOTTOM LINE
===============================================================================

Per-event parental origin is recoverable for a MINORITY of the 1,189 regions,
and the minority is well defined:

RECOVERABLE
 - Losses >= ~1 Mb in embryos whose egg donor has one anchor array (cumulus or a
   PB1+PB2 pair), any cell type. This is the large majority of the 871 losses if
   an anchor exists per donor group, since anchoring does not require the anchor
   and the event to share an array.
 - Mitotic losses in embryos with >=3, preferably >=4, arrayed sibling cells,
   with NO anchor at all, by construction A.
 - Whole-chromosome and >=20 Mb segmental gains, anchored arm only.

NOT RECOVERABLE
 - Any event below about 750-780 kb even in the best case (6-8 arrayed cells,
   scaled het rule), and below about 1.3-1.7 Mb at the median 3-4 cells.
   Detected, breakpointed to 60 kb, and refused a parental call. This is a hard
   information limit at 3.7-6.7 independent markers/Mb, not a modelling
   shortfall.
 - Gains < ~10-20 Mb, even anchored.
 - Meiotic events in embryos with <3 arrayed cells and no anchor.
 - Anything in a donor group with no anchor and only one egg donor: the 2PN
   structure makes cross-donor reconstruction unavailable there, and without an
   anchor those calls stay unnamed. Relative assignment is still valid and still
   answers the aggregate question at a worse MDE (0.642 at 30x10).

The single highest-value action is not a modelling change. It is arraying one
cumulus or PB1+PB2 sample per egg donor. That is a small number of arrays, it
converts unnamed to named for every embryo in the group, and it moves the
minimum detectable effect from 0.642-0.779 (unnamed, never reaching the floor)
to 0.572-0.676 (named, reaching 0.60 at 20 donors x 10 events).
