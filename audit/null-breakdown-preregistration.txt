THE SEGMENT NULL'S BREAKDOWN, AND WHAT THE OBLIGATE SET CAN NAME WITHOUT IT

Committed before the run. One pass, seven predictions, and the first three are an external review's
rather than mine.

THE DIAGNOSIS BEING TESTED

`externalNull` (segments.ts:233) is the MEDIAN per-chromosome absence rate over the sample's other
autosomes. Its own comment says a median is chosen so that several events cannot collectively raise
the bar. A median's breakdown point is 50 percent. By the published karyotype, 8 of the 31 public
cells carry 11 or more abnormal autosomes of 22, so on those the median IS an affected chromosome.

`scanChromosome` then rejects before scoring: `if (rate <= nullRate) continue` (segments.ts:274),
ahead of the likelihood ratio. So on a cell whose median absence rate sits at the level a real
event produces, every window on every chromosome is discarded untested, whatever the marker count.

A real single-parent loss does not drive absence to 1.0. At markers where the mother is homozygous
and her copy is gone, the sample reads an opposite homozygote only where the father transmitted the
other allele. The review puts that at about 0.100 from a fitted allele-frequency spectrum. An
independent figure from this project agrees: paternal pronuclei read 11.6 to 12.0 percent against
the egg donors, which is the same quantity measured on real arrays.

If both halves hold, one defect explains three findings: chr16 finding nothing with 14,000 markers,
the 35 contested windows, and the segments on 8 cells resting on a reference the sample cannot
supply.

A CORRECTION TO THE REVIEW, FOUND BEFORE RUNNING

The review says the discriminator for a both-parents window already exists as the survivor share,
and gives it as `max(onlyA, onlyB) / (onlyA + onlyB)`, which is 0.5 under symmetric dropout at any
rate and 1.0 on a real single-parent loss. The symmetry argument is exact: the array cannot know
which allele came from which parent, so dropout cannot be parent-specific.

The shipped code does not compute that. `hetCall` uses `onlyA / informative`, a share of ALL
informative markers, and `SURVIVOR_MIN = 0.70` is calibrated against THAT denominator: 595
autosomes, 105 hand-called losses never under 0.735, 490 intact never reaching 0.627. The two
differ wherever heterozygous calls survive, because `informative` includes them and
`onlyA + onlyB` does not. The review's version is invariant to dropout; the shipped one is not.

So the tally exists and the calibrated threshold belongs to a different quantity. Both are computed
below and neither is assumed to be the better one.

THE DATA

  GSE148488, Zuccaro et al., Cell 2020;183(6):1650-1664. The 31 cells with both parents arrayed and
  a by-eye karyotype in Table S3. Band coordinates from the UCSC hg19 cytoBand track. No simulation.

THE PREDICTIONS

Theirs, on the diagnosis:

  P1. On GSM4472423, GSM4552385 and GSM4552386, `externalNull` for the MATERNAL run on chr16 is at
      or above about 0.10, the rate a true maternal loss produces.
  P2. On those cells the 16q23.1-tel window's maternal absence rate is near that same figure and
      fails `rate <= nullRate`, so it is discarded before the likelihood ratio is computed.
  P3. On the six contested cells both runs' nulls are raised, and the contested windows' two
      absence rates are approximately EQUAL rather than both near the event rate.

      THE FALSIFIER, AND IT IS THEIRS: if the contested windows are strongly asymmetric, the
      dropout reading is wrong, their answer to the both-parents question falls, and so does the
      case for replacing the null. Recorded, not rescued.

Mine, on what can be named without the null:

  S1. On the four cells carrying a hand-called `del(6q)(pat)` that the heterozygosity ceiling
      refuses, chr6 reads a survivor share naming PATERNAL under at least one of the two
      normalisations. These cells are refused and `contribution` is not cleared by the refusal, so
      this is a parent call available today on an array the tool reports nothing for.
  S2. Measured directly from the two parental arrays, with no spectrum assumed, the rate at which a
      sample carrying one parent's allele alone reads an opposite homozygote against the other
      parent is between 0.08 and 0.14.
  S3. The opposite-homozygote marker count inside 16q23.1-tel is reported against
      `MIN_INFORMATIVE = 200`. The review scales it to about 171 and flags the figure as an
      estimate. This decides whether the obligate set can speak at window scale at all.
  S4. The two normalisations part company on the contested windows. The review's reads near 0.5
      there, by its symmetry argument. The shipped one reads low for an unrelated reason, because
      surviving heterozygous calls sit in its denominator. If they do not part company, the
      distinction does not matter and the shipped threshold can be reused.

WHAT IS NOT BEING DECIDED HERE

The replacement null. The review declines to propose one and gives the reason: on a cell with 15 of
22 autosomes affected, no robust estimate of location over the sample's own chromosomes can work,
so the replacement may have to be parent-referenced rather than within-sample. Candidates have
different failure modes and the choice is a separate pre-registration scored against the
595-autosome truth. Nothing is changed in this round on the strength of these numbers alone.
