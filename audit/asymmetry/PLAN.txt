PARENT-OF-ORIGIN ASYMMETRY: WHAT THE DESIGN MUST DO
===================================================
Target question: do breakpoints and losses affect the paternal and maternal genomes differentially
or equally. Design constraints below come from an external statistical review; measurements marked
MEASURED HERE were made on the laboratory arrays (GPL28377, 825,657 markers, 884 exports).

THE BINDING CONSTRAINT IS NOT POWER, IT IS DETECTION SENSITIVITY
----------------------------------------------------------------
If maternal and maternal events are detected with unequal sensitivity, true parity reads as
asymmetry. The exchange rate, at one cell per embryo, is that a 7.7% maternal detection shortfall
alone manufactures an apparent paternal fraction of 0.52. At 3 cells it takes 19.2%, at 8 cells
46.6%. So arraying more cells per embryo is a BIAS control, not only a precision measure, and on
single-cell-per-embryo material no claim is supportable at all.

  Claimable floor: no effect below paternal fraction 0.60, and nothing on 1-cell-per-embryo material.
  Roughly 20-30 oocyte donors before a null means anything; at 10 donors the MDE is 0.64-0.70.

UNIT OF ANALYSIS
----------------
The EVENT, deduplicated within embryo. Not the array (replicate arrays per cell exist here) and not
the cell (a meiotic event is one event seen in every blastomere). Primary test is a binomial GLMM
with donor and embryo-within-donor random intercepts, by likelihood ratio.

THE MITOTIC/MEIOTIC LABEL IS LATENT AND ITS ERRORS ARE PARENT-DEPENDENT
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
An event missed in some cells is called mitotic. At true parity with a 0.70 sensitivity ratio, the
apparent paternal fraction splits 0.743 meiotic against 0.476 mitotic, a 26.8 point gap that is
entirely artefact. At equal sensitivity the gap is 0.000. The class-stratified analysis is
therefore the MOST vulnerable, not the least, and is exploratory only.

THE NULL IS NOT 50:50, AND NOT ONE NULL
----------------------------------------
Direction flips between classes: meiotic whole-chromosome runs about 0.10 paternal across two
independent studies, meiotic segmental 0.58-0.71 paternal. For mitotic segmental, the target class,
there is NO published parental split in any species, because haplotype methods define a mitotic
event as one with a normal biparental pattern and so cannot assign its parent. That gap is what
this work fills, and it means no informative prior can be borrowed. Pooling classes can return a
null that is an artefact of pooling.

RECONSTRUCTING THE MOTHER IS DISQUALIFYING
-------------------------------------------
Cross-donor sharing recovers the FATHER well (99.28% over 65.6% of markers from 14 embryos across
4 donors). It fails for the mother, who is shared only within her own sibship, and the failure runs
in the direction being measured: substituting a reconstructed mother turns a true
log2(maternal/paternal) absence ratio of -0.40 into +2.18. The mechanism is that reconstruction
inflates the informative marker count 1.27x to 1.84x by admitting markers where she is actually
heterozygous, and those read as maternal absence.

  Use a maternal array where one exists. Where none exists, report the embryo as unlabelled and
  state the fraction. Never mix reconstructed and genotyped donors silently.
  Polar bodies are the correct substitute for a maternal array, not sibling reconstruction.
  PB1 is 2C, not haploid: it carries both sister chromatids of the non-transmitted homologue. A
  PB1+PB2 pair observes 3 chromatids of the tetrad and is worth 3.66-4.06 independent 1C products
  for reconstruction, while counting as ONE meiosis for event-level inference.

MEASURED HERE: HOMOZYGOTE DIRECTION SKEW ON GPL28377
-----------------------------------------------------
12 laboratory arrays sampled across studies and stages, every 7th marker:

  AA-vs-BB homozygote skew   median -0.1797   mean -0.2054   sd 0.0502   range -0.3271 .. -0.1635

BB is called consistently more often than AA, on every array, at every stage. THIS IS NOT YET
EVIDENCE OF A BIASED PARENTAL SPLIT and must not be used as one. The measurement conflates two
things and cannot separate them without a parent array:

  1. Marker composition. The two obligate-het orientations are not equinumerous. This does NOT
     bias the parental split: one obligate-het marker detects loss of EITHER parent, so the
     informative denominator is identical for both directions.
  2. A genuine direction-of-call asymmetry, which DOES propagate, because paternal and maternal
     loss are read out through opposite homozygous states at any given marker. On a public series
     with a known trio this is 0.0297 (expected homozygote recovered at 0.9134 in the AA direction
     against 0.9430 in the BB direction) - an order of magnitude smaller than the raw skew above.

  TO DO: repeat against a genotyped parent and haploid controls on this platform to separate the
  two, and feed only component 2 into the ascertainment bound. Do not re-centre the null on a
  marker-count ratio.

TWO PREMISES TO VERIFY BEFORE THE CALIBRATION ARM IS USED
----------------------------------------------------------
The chr16 and chrX arm is a symmetry control only if those guides are NOT allele specific. The same
laboratory's published headline result is allele-SPECIFIC chromosome removal after Cas9 cleavage.
If any calibration guide is allele specific the expected 50:50 split fails and the control inverts.
Check the guide sequences.

And the arm is weaker than hoped even if the premise holds: at 20 induced events the 95% interval
on the sensitivity ratio is 0.354-2.827. Below roughly 115 events against 150 spontaneous ones, a
point correction adds more variance than it removes bias. Use the arm as a BOUND and a sensitivity
analysis, never as a routine correction.

COHORT STRUCTURE: TWO GROUPS THAT MUST BE ANALYSED SEPARATELY
--------------------------------------------------------------
Stated by the laboratory, and it decides which parental route is available:

  2PN-THAWED embryos (Turocy, much of Jerabek)   ONE egg donor and ONE sperm per group.
    Cross-donor sharing cannot identify the father here: there is only one mother per group, so
    nothing distinguishes a shared paternal haplotype from a shared maternal one. This group needs
    a real parental array, from polar bodies, cumulus, or sperm.
  MII-plus-SPERM embryos (some Jerabek, Robles)  DIFFERENT egg donors, SAME sperm.
    This is the structure the cross-donor construction requires, and the one where paternal
    reconstruction is sound.

A GENOTYPED FATHER EXISTS FOR PART OF THE SET. The EYS sperm donor is on this platform as bulk
gDNA in four replicates, GSM4472397-4472400 of GSE148488, and two of those already ship in this
repo as example data. Where EYS was the sperm source, the paternal side needs no inference at all.

THE MATERNAL REFERENCE IS ONE SAMPLE, AND IT IS USABLE
-------------------------------------------------------
There is exactly one cumulus sample in the whole set: 14801-05, experiment 8. Measured against
the known bulk gDNA on this platform, it is bulk-like rather than single-cell:

  GSM4472397  sperm donor bulk gDNA      call 0.954   het 0.168
  14801-05    cumulus, maternal somatic  call 0.950   het 0.145

NOTE THE PLATFORM RANGE. A diploid genome reads about 0.15-0.17 heterozygous on GPL28377, not the
0.30 typical of a denser common-SNP array. A threshold carried over from another platform will
call every diploid here haploid.

Experiment 14801 is therefore the one complete unit in the set: a maternal somatic reference, a
polar body, three MII oocyte biopsies, isolated zygote nuclei, and a genotyped father if EYS was
the sperm source. 38 arrays. Whatever is built should be built and checked here first.

FOUR ARRAYS IN THAT EXPERIMENT ARE FAILURES and must be excluded rather than modelled:
14801-20, -23, -27 and -38 read 0.53-0.56 heterozygous at 0.45-0.59 call rate. No genome does
that; it is amplification failure or mixture.

COHORT ARITHMETIC
-----------------
The three public series total 459 arrays (135 + 166 + 158). 884 probe exports are held locally, so
the difference is unpublished material or duplication across folders. Reconcile before any methods
section quotes a sample count.
