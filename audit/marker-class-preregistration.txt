WHICH MARKER CLASS CARRIES ONE CONTRIBUTION AGAINST TWO, WITH ONE PARENT LOADED

Committed before the run. Steps 1 and 2 of an external review's order, measured together because
they need the same pass.

THE CLAIM BEING TESTED

`oneParentOrigin.ts` keeps markers where the loaded parent is HOMOZYGOUS and excludes the rest,
and its docstring says a marker where that parent is heterozygous "cannot say anything at all".
For the three-way copy question that is right: at a parent-heterozygous marker both loss
hypotheses predict the same thing, so they cannot be separated from each other there.

But the laboratory's question on a chaotic cell is narrower than three ways. It is whether a second
parental contribution is present on this chromosome, which is two hypotheses, and at a
parent-heterozygous marker those two separate on heterozygosity alone:

  parent homozygous:    two contributions  het = q(1 - ado) + d,  q = 0.1217
  parent heterozygous:  two contributions  het = 0.5(1 - ado) + d
  either class:         one contribution   het = d

So the signal is 4.1 times larger in the excluded class, and it no longer depends on the drop-in
parameter, which an external review shows is measured on bulk material and reads 1.66 times higher
on uniparental amplified arrays.

An external review measured this on 175 chromosome observations against a bulk reference parent and
reported a clean separation in the excluded class where the shipped class overlaps. That measurement
was on a different experiment with truth labels the review assigned itself from the marker channels.
This run repeats it where the truth is the authors' published by-eye karyotype.

THE DATA

GSE148488, the 31 cells with both parents arrayed and a karyotype in Table S3. Both parents are
loaded, so the measurement runs twice per chromosome, once with each parent as the loaded one, and a
chromosome contributes to the one-contribution class for the parent whose copy the hand says is
gone and to the two-contribution class when the hand reads it normal. 595 autosomes.

THE PREDICTIONS

  V1. The parent-HETEROZYGOUS class separates one contribution from two with no overlap, and the
      parent-HOMOZYGOUS class overlaps. This is the review's central claim and the reason to run.
  V2. Their figures reproduce to within the difference between the two corpora: parent-homozygous
      one-contribution reaching about 0.083 against two-contribution falling to about 0.034, so an
      overlap; parent-heterozygous one-contribution bounded near 0.21 against two-contribution
      above about 0.28.
  V3. The shipped boundaries `ONE_PARENT_HAPLOID_MAX = 0.065` and `ONE_PARENT_DIPLOID_MIN = 0.085`
      fall inside the parent-homozygous overlap, so a large share of chromosomes the hand reads as
      normal are called uniparental by the shipped class.
  V4. `MIN_INFORMATIVE = 200` admits chromosomes that are not being measured. At least one autosome
      in this corpus passes it with an informative count an order of magnitude below its siblings
      and reports a heterozygous rate outside any biological range. The review found one at 312
      markers against a typical 4,000.

  FALSIFIED IF V1 fails: if the excluded class does not separate better than the shipped one, the
  review's answer to the 0.289 problem is wrong, the marker class is not the limit, and nothing is
  changed on the strength of it.

STEP 2, MEASURED IN THE SAME PASS

The informative floor. Reported per chromosome: the informative count in each class, that count as a
FRACTION of the same chromosome's called markers, and the heterozygous rate. The question is whether
a fractional floor separates the chromosomes that are not being measured from the ones with extreme
values, where an absolute floor of 200 does not. No threshold is proposed here; the distribution
decides the form, as it did for the segment null.

WHAT IS NOT IN SCOPE

Any change to the three-way caller. The excluded class cannot separate the two loss hypotheses from
each other and this file does not pretend otherwise. What it can do is answer the narrower question,
and whether that is worth shipping as a second channel is decided after the separation is measured.

The review's own warning is recorded: this class answers "is there a second contribution", not
"whose". Two gynogenetic samples in its corpus read 0.341 and 0.373, indistinguishable from a
biparental 0.397 on the mean alone. So a high reading in this class is not evidence of a paternal
contribution, and anything built on it must say so.

================================================================================================
THE RESULT, WRITTEN AFTER THE RUN

Nothing above this line was changed. Measured on both corpora: the public one where the truth is a
published karyotype, and the laboratory series where the review took its figures.

V1 FAILS. BOTH CLASSES OVERLAP ON THE PUBLIC CORPUS.

  class                  truth     n      min   median      max
  parent-HOM (shipped)   one     109   0.0208   0.0789   0.4606
  parent-HOM (shipped)   two     954   0.0604   0.1141   0.6775
                                       overlap 0.4003
  parent-HET (excluded)  one     109   0.0743   0.1488   0.4320
  parent-HET (excluded)  two     954   0.1334   0.3352   0.7460
                                       overlap 0.2986

The excluded class does separate BETTER: a ratio of medians of 2.25 against 1.45, which is the
direction the review's mechanism predicts, since it reads 0.5(1-ado) where the shipped class reads
q(1-ado). But neither class gives a clean boundary, and raising the informative floor to 1,000 in
both classes changes nothing.

V2 FAILS. The review reported parent-homozygous one-contribution reaching 0.0834 against
two-contribution falling to 0.0343, and parent-heterozygous bounded at 0.2057 against 0.2849, a
clean gap. Those do not reproduce. Its corpus was 175 chromosome-observations of a different
experiment with truth labels it assigned itself from the marker channels; this is 1,063
observations against the authors' published reading.

V3 FAILS, AND IN THE OPPOSITE DIRECTION, on the public corpus. The review predicted that the
shipped boundaries misclassify most biparental chromosomes. Measured here, 4 of 954 chromosomes the
hand reads as normal fall below `ONE_PARENT_HAPLOID_MAX`, which is 0.4 percent, and 0 of 954 on the
excluded class.

BUT W1 AND W2 REPRODUCE EXACTLY ON THE LABORATORY SERIES, AND THE READING IS THE DIFFERENCE.

The opposite-homozygote rate of every sample of that series against its measured reference array
reproduces the review's figures to three decimals: 7 consistent at 0.0017 to 0.0075, 1 intermediate
at 0.0263, 8 at 0.064 to 0.097, and 2 arrays with no usable DNA. And its 111 of 154 reproduces
exactly: 72.1 percent of the chromosomes of the 7 consistent samples fall below the haploid maximum.

That is not a defect. Those 7 samples read heterozygosity of 0.040 to 0.064, which is haploid: they
are single pronuclei, not embryos. A haploid product has one genome, so calling its chromosomes
uniparental is the correct answer and 72 percent is the correct figure. The 8 at 0.064 to 0.097 are
haploid products of the OTHER parent, which is what this project already calls them. The review
read a pronuclei experiment as an embryo experiment.

On the parent-HET class the same 154 chromosomes give 90 of 154, so the excluded class is WORSE at
recognising haploid material, which is the opposite of V1 again.

V4 HOLDS, AND IT REPRODUCES TO FOUR DECIMALS.

  the series median autosome carries 5,249 parent-heterozygous markers
  one chromosome carries 312, which is 5.9 percent of that
  it reports 0.5514 heterozygous on the parent-homozygous class and 0.7147 on the other
  it clears the absolute floor of 200 with 1,605 informative markers

The review's figures were 312, 0.5514 and 0.7147. Exact. Its siblings' same chromosome carries
1,710 to 1,960, so the next-thinnest observation in the series sits at 32.6 percent of the median.
On the public corpus nothing comparable occurs.

STEP 2: WHAT THE RIGHT DENOMINATOR IS, AND THE FIRST ANSWER WAS WRONG

A fraction of the sample's median AUTOSOME was tried first and a regression on the public corpus
refused chr22 of one cell for carrying 17 percent of what the median autosome carries. chr22 is
simply a small chromosome. A count compared across chromosomes of different sizes cannot work.

Three candidates measured per chromosome on the laboratory series:

  informative / called            min 0.7810   median 0.8185   the bad chromosome reads 0.7891
  informative / markers on array  min 0.1377   median 0.7031   the bad chromosome reads 0.1377
  called / markers on array       min 0.1745   median 0.8638   the bad chromosome reads 0.1745

The informative share of called markers is unremarkable on it. The chromosome was barely genotyped:
2,034 called of the 11,655 the array holds. The CALL RATE separates it with nothing between 0.175
and 0.813, and `CALL_COLLAPSE = 0.60` already exists in parentage.ts for exactly this question, so
the fix adds no constant.

THE CONTROL FAILED, AND IS RECORDED RATHER THAN EXPLAINED AWAY.

This file said the public corpus would be unaffected. It is not: 63 of 682 chromosomes lose their
contribution call. Every one is on a cell whose chromosome call rate is 0.30 to 0.58, below the
0.60 the project already defines as collapsed, and the cells are the degraded ones: two are refused
at the genome level already, and on the one accepted chaotic cell the refused chromosomes are
chr16, chr17 and chr20, which are three of the four that produced self-contradictory contested
segments. So the change is consistent with the rest of the pipeline and tightens the population
that was producing contradictions. It is still a behaviour change the control did not anticipate.


THE REST OF THE SUITE, RE-RUN ON THE PUBLIC CORPUS WITH THE FLOOR IN PLACE

The control above failed: 63 of 682 chromosomes lose their contribution call. So every scored
figure was re-measured to see what that cost.

  figure                                    before      after
  detection, deletion-bearing events        15 of 22    15 of 22
    paternal                                10 of 14    10 of 14
    maternal                                 5 of 8      5 of 8
  attribution, the run channel              15 of 15    15 of 15
  attribution, the window share             11 of 11    11 of 11
  copy-number segments on an admitted cell         0           0
  refused cells admitted as a chaotic genome       0           0

Nothing scored moved. The 63 refusals cost no detection and no attribution, which is what a gate
should do when it is refusing chromosomes that were not being measured. The parental-absence segment
count and the contested count did move, 69 to 75 and 34 to 38, and those are the reciprocal-overlap
and detectability changes of 5.43.0 rather than this floor.
