WHY THIS DATA

  Zuccaro et al. separated the two pronuclei of a fertilised human zygote by micromanipulation
  and arrayed each one alone. A "paternal nucleus" sample therefore contains one parental
  genome and a "maternal nucleus" the other, established at the bench and not by any
  inference this tool makes. That is the strongest ground truth available for the question
  Syngamy answers: it is the answer, physically separated, before any statistic is applied.

  The series also carries four arrays of one sperm donor and eight of four egg donors, which
  supply identity and unrelated controls from the same platform and the same laboratory.

HOW TO REPRODUCE

  1. Download the 27 supplementary files listed under SOURCES below from GEO.
  2. node --experimental-strip-types --max-old-space-size=8192 audit/cases.ts <raw> <out>
  3. node --experimental-strip-types audit/report.ts <out>

  The harness drives web/src/ingest.ts, web/src/parentage.ts and web/src/syngamyPdf.ts, which
  is the same code the browser runs. Only the file plumbing differs: a gzipped file off disk
  rather than a dropped one, and favicon.svg read rather than fetched. Every PDF in this
  folder was produced by the same builder as the Report (PDF) button.

THE FOUR REFUSALS, IN FULL
================================================================================================

None of these is a wrong answer. In each the tool declined to name a class and said why. That
distinction is the reason the table above separates "refused" from "incorrect": a refusal
costs a reader a result, and a wrong answer costs them the experiment.

pat-GSM4774684  (paternal nucleus isolated from 2PN zygote 5)
No chrY, yet the parent's alleles are present on chrX as well as the autosomes: an X-bearing
               sperm. This is the case a chrY test cannot call.

pat-GSM4774686  (paternal nucleus isolated from 2PN zygote 7)
Call rate 53.8% is below 60%, where erroneous heterozygous calls become common. Zygosity is read
               from the heterozygous band, so it is not reported here and the genome cannot be
               separated into uniparental or biparental. The presence or absence of this
               parent's contribution is unaffected: that is Mendelian and does not rest on
               heterozygous calls.

pat-GSM4774687  (paternal nucleus isolated from 2PN zygote 8)
Call rate 59.1% is below 60%, where erroneous heterozygous calls become common. Zygosity is read
               from the heterozygous band, so it is not reported here and the genome cannot be
               separated into uniparental or biparental. The presence or absence of this
               parent's contribution is unaffected: that is Mendelian and does not rest on
               heterozygous calls.

egg-GSM4472408  (genomic DNA egg donor D)

The first three are isolated paternal pronuclei, which are haploid and therefore homozygous by
construction. Each shows a heterozygous BAF band of 18 to 27 per cent, which a haploid genome
cannot produce, and each sits below the 60 per cent call rate at which erroneous heterozygous
calls become common (Natesan et al. 2014, Genet Med 16:838-845). The tool excludes all three on
its own call-rate gate before any call is made. On the paternal axis it was right about all
three anyway: absence at 0.44x, 0.49x and 0.45x of the ceiling, so the paternal genome was
correctly detected as present in each. What it withholds is zygosity, and with it the split
between androgenetic and biparental, because that rests on the heterozygous calls the gate has
just declared untrustworthy.

The fourth is egg donor D, which passes every gate but is the noisiest of the eight donor
arrays: an 81.9 per cent call rate against 96 to 98 per cent for the others, and a 21.1 per
cent heterozygous band against 16.3 to 16.5. Its own noise can account for 4.33 per cent
absence and it shows 4.69, which is 1.08x: inside the band between the ceiling and three times
it, where nothing is called. The seven other egg-donor arrays, all unrelated to the sperm
donor in the same way, are called correctly at 4.8x to 7.7x.

================================================================================================
WHAT THIS AUDIT CHANGED IN THE TOOL
================================================================================================

The first run of this audit returned 3 incorrect calls, not 0. The three paternal pronuclei
above were reported as biparental: their spurious heterozygous band pushed zygosity to
"diploid", and a diploid genome carrying the sperm donor's alleles is by definition
biparental. The tool had already excluded those samples on its call-rate gate, and its
het-to-hom gate had already printed "SUSPENDED: below 60% call rate erroneous heterozygous
calls are common" - and then the classifier used the heterozygous band anyway. It knew the
measurement was invalid and used it.

That is now fixed. Below the published call-rate floor, zygosity is withheld and the class
falls to unclear with the reason stated. Absence is unaffected and still called, because it is
Mendelian: a homozygous father must transmit his allele whatever the call rate. The change
turned three wrong answers into three refusals and left every other case identical.

================================================================================================
LIMITS OF THIS AUDIT
================================================================================================

  One laboratory, one platform, one sperm donor. Everything here is Affymetrix Axiom UK
  Biobank data from the Egli lab. It says nothing about Illumina arrays, about other
  amplification chemistries, or about how the tool behaves on a second sperm donor.

  The pronuclei are single nuclei, which is harder material than a trophectoderm biopsy of
  five to ten cells. That cuts both ways: it is a stress test, and it is not representative of
  the call rates a clinical biopsy would give.

  26 scored cases is not enough to claim an accuracy figure. 22 correct and 0 incorrect has a
  95% lower bound near 87% on the proportion that are not incorrect, which is worth stating
  and is not worth rounding up to "accurate". No confidence percentage appears on any report
  this tool produces, for the same reason.

  Ground truth for the maternal pronuclei covers the paternal side only. Which egg donor
  contributed each one is not in the GEO record, so that half is scored on internal
  consistency: the tool had to pick one of four, and it picked one every time, with the
  runner-up 10 to 18 times further away. That is evidence, but it is not a labelled answer.

  Research use only. Not a clinical diagnostic.
