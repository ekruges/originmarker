0. HEADLINE
===========
Your instinct is right and your current behaviour is wrong, but not for the reason you
think. "Always call with a calibrated confidence" IS achievable on this material, and I can
show the reliability diagram. The thing that has to be replaced is not the refusal policy
and not the estimator. It is the assumption, currently built into the tool, that ORIGIN is a
question you can answer while CLASS is unresolved.

Five results, in order of how much they change the build.

1. A calibrated parental-origin posterior is reachable, measured, on all four material
   classes including single blastomeres. Expected calibration error after leave-one-array-out
   recalibration: 0.0027 bulk, 0.0039 single ESC WGA, 0.0072 TE, 0.0030 blastomere. The
   recalibration map never sees the array it is tested on. This is the deliverable you asked
   for and it exists.

2. THE CORRECTION THAT CHANGES THE SCIENCE. The sign of the parent-allele shift names a
   parent only GIVEN the copy-number class, because gain inverts the map that loss and
   CNN-LOH share. Your own audit already says 89-100% of detected events on TE and
   blastomere resolve an origin but NOT a class. Those two facts together mean the current
   tool reports an origin in exactly the regime where the sign does not identify a parent.
   Measured consequence: a true low-fraction GAIN, scored the way the tool scores it now,
   names the WRONG parent 55.1-58.0% of the time at f=0.05 - worse than a coin flip, and
   systematically so, because the error is a sign inversion rather than noise. Of all wrong
   calls that land in the top two confidence bands, 55.7% are gains.
   The fix is not a better estimator. It is to marginalise the class into the posterior and
   let the intensity channel supply what direction information exists. Origin and class are
   currently separate fields in your report; they are not separable quantities.

3. Your posterior, before recalibration, is UNDER-confident, not over-confident. Every
   reliability curve sits above the diagonal (ECE 0.033-0.059). This is the opposite of the
   failure you built the refusal policy to prevent, and it means the withholding threshold
   at f=0.30 is throwing away calls that would have been honest.

4. The f=0.30 blanket threshold should go, and be replaced by the band the posterior lands
   in. This also settles your "always call" question in your favour: because every band
   including the weakest is calibrated within itself, a number can be attached everywhere
   except one specific cell (item 2 above) and the structural refusals. Measured on blastomeres - your worst material, the one with no floor at any fraction
   on the shipped statistic - 24.1% of events land in a band whose measured accuracy is
   0.9971, and 43.0% land in the weakest band, which is itself calibrated at 0.618. A single
   fraction threshold cannot express that, because f explains only 51.7% of the variance in
   achievable confidence; material, array identity, marker count and class carry the rest.

5. Two things you were told, and one thing in your own notes, are wrong. The MII-to-SPH rule
   is not general (MII WITH a crossover gives BPH tracts distal to the centromere, so BPH
   does not cleanly separate meiotic from mitotic). PH-trace is low-pass WGS, not a SNP
   array, so its AUC 0.999 and its 30-SNP informativity floor do not transfer to your marker
   budget. And the pericentromeric region is not as hopeless as "2.5 to 121 markers" implies
   - it carries 578 markers and 63 informative het markers at +/-2 Mb on a real high-call-rate
   array here - but it is hopeless for a different and firmer reason, given in section 2.


1. WHAT THE CONFIDENCE SCORE SHOULD BE
======================================

1.1 The score
-------------
A posterior probability that the affected copy came from the parent you did NOT load,
marginalised over BOTH nuisance quantities: the mosaic fraction f AND the copy-number class.

  P(other parent | S, L) = sum_class sum_f  p(class) p(f) N(S; -sg(class) d(class,f), sd_S)
                                                          x N(L; dl(class,f), sd_L)
                           / [same, both sides]

  S     = self-referenced displacement of the parent-allele share over the event window
  L     = self-referenced displacement of window log2R
  d     = the audit's pooled displacement, f/(4-2f) loss, f/2 CNN-LOH, f/(4+2f) gain
  dl    = log2((2-f)/2) loss, 0 CNN-LOH, log2((2+f)/2) gain
  sg    = -1 for loss and CNN-LOH, +1 for gain           <- THE INVERSION
  sd_S  = self-referenced scale from the array's own genome at the same window size and the
          same window composition
  sd_L  = the same for the intensity channel

Why a posterior and not a likelihood ratio or a bare band: you said a user will compare two
rows in a table. Only a probability is comparable across material, class and marker count
without carrying its own reference. An LR of 12 means different things at k=100 and k=800; a
posterior of 0.92 does not.

Why marginalise f rather than plug in f-hat: the plug-in form is measurably worse calibrated
in exactly the region you care about (ECE 0.0320 vs 0.0126 on TE, 0.0219 vs 0.0055 on
blastomere), because at low f the magnitude is estimated with error comparable to itself and
the plug-in treats it as known. Marginalising is not a refinement, it is the difference
between a calibrated and an uncalibrated score.

1.2 The recalibration step, and why it is not cheating
-----------------------------------------------------
The raw posterior above is under-confident. Fix it with an isotonic (monotone) map from raw
posterior to calibrated posterior, fitted per material class. Monotone means it cannot change
any call - it changes only the number attached to the call. Measured: accuracy identical to
4 decimal places (0.9116 -> 0.9113 bulk, 0.8515 -> 0.8515 single ESC, 0.8085 -> 0.8094 TE,
0.7878 -> 0.7884 blastomere), ECE down by a factor of 8-20.

Fit it leave-one-array-out. Every number I quote is from a map that never saw the array it
was scored on. This is the difference between demonstrating calibration and asserting it, and
it is the thing you should require of any threshold that ships.

1.3 Where the score is still NOT calibrated, and what to do
-----------------------------------------------------------
A single pooled map is calibrated on the pooled input space but NOT within class. Stated
minus measured confidence, pooled map, positive = over-confident:

  material          CNN-LOH    gain     loss
  bulk               +0.027   +0.025   -0.049
  single ESC WGA     +0.053   +0.005   -0.063
  TE                 +0.064   -0.010   -0.059
  blastomere         +0.068   +0.001   -0.068

CNN-LOH rows are over-confident by 2.7-6.8 points and loss rows under-confident by about
the same. That is a real defect: a reader comparing a CNN-LOH row against a loss row at the
same stated confidence is being misled in opposite directions.

Two candidate fixes, one works and one does not.
  - Stratify the map on OBSERVABLES only (|z| of the intensity channel, three strata). Does
    NOT fix it: CNN-LOH gap stays +0.029 to +0.075. Measured, do not bother.
  - Fit the map PER CLASS, on rows where the class is resolved. Fixes it completely: ECE
    0.0002-0.0050 and every gap within +/-0.0015 across all 12 material-by-class cells.
So: per-class calibration is required, which means the class must be resolved to state a
per-class-calibrated confidence. This is the same conclusion as section 0 item 2 arriving
from the calibration side rather than the algebra side.

2. TIMING AND MECHANISM: WHAT IS REACHABLE AND WHAT IS NOT
==========================================================

2.1 Your three-way question
---------------------------
You asked whether these separate: post-fertilisation mitotic duplication, a chromosome that
arrived duplicated from the father, and a de novo segmental change in the gamete.

Partly. Your expectation is right in one leg and wrong in another, and the wrong leg is
one you were told.

  BPH (both parental homologues)  -> meiosis I non-disjunction. CONFIRMED.
  SPH (single parental homologue) -> post-zygotic duplication. CONFIRMED.
  SPH                            <- meiosis II. NOT SUPPORTED AS A GENERAL RULE.

MII non-disjunction with a crossover produces BPH tracts DISTAL to the centromere; only MII
WITHOUT recombination gives chromosome-wide SPH. The MII/mitotic collapse is acknowledged
explicitly in the primary literature and nobody separates the two on genotype alone. So
BPH-vs-SPH is not "meiotic vs mitotic"; it is "MI (and recombinant MII, distally) vs
(non-recombinant MII or mitotic)".

  APCAD2 2025: "SPH trisomies are expected to be predominantly mitotic in origin ... even
  though a meiotic segregation error in meiosis II without recombination cannot be excluded."

2.2 Why the pericentromeric route is closed here, and it is not the marker count
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You were told 2.5-121 markers and therefore hopeless. The marker count is not the binding
constraint; I measured it. On a real high-call-rate array in your corpus, against UCSC hg19
centromere gaps (n=22 autosomes):

  window        markers (median)   informative het markers (median, min-max)
  +/-0.5 Mb            74                 6.5   (0-22)
  +/-1 Mb             212                19     (0-58)
  +/-2 Mb             578                63     (0-103)
  +/-5 Mb           1,812               209     (6-327)

63 informative markers at +/-2 Mb is thin but not hopeless. Three harder constraints close
it anyway:

  (i) Recombination suppression is sex-specific and asymmetric, which is the useful part and
      also the trap. deCODE 1-Mb map (UCSC hg19 recombRate): genome-wide 1.598 cM/Mb female,
      0.982 male. Pericentromeric +/-2 Mb: 0.660 female, 0.138 male - that is 0.413x the
      female genome average but 0.140x the male. Female/male ratio rises from 1.63
      genome-wide to 4.79 at the centromere. So the "retained heterozygosity = MI" rule is
      much safer for paternal events than maternal ones, and the informative window is
      wider on the male side. Nobody in your notes has used this; it is a real asymmetry
      you could exploit for paternal-origin trisomy staging specifically.
  (ii) Reverse segregation. Both homologues separate sister chromatids at MI and non-sisters
      at MII. Measured across 23 complete female meioses, it was the most frequent
      non-canonical pattern (26 cases), and in 20 of 26 the copy number was NORMAL, so it is
      invisible to any copy-number method. A reverse-segregated chromosome carries
      pericentromeric heterozygosity that the classical rule reads as MI when the physical
      MI event was equational. This is not a power problem. It is the rule being wrong on a
      non-trivial fraction of chromosomes, and no amount of marker density fixes it.
  (iii) Inconclusive rate on real few-cell material with BOTH parents available: 22.9% (8 of
      35 meiotic-origin triploids). You should expect 20-25% abstention on this axis even in
      the two-parent case.

RECOMMENDATION: do not ship MI/MII staging. Ship BPH-positive vs "CN3, not BPH", which is
what your own audit already supports (AUC 1.000 from k=400, SPH contamination 0.0000), and
state that the not-BPH class pools non-recombinant MII with mitotic. Report the
recombination-suppression asymmetry as a reason the call is better for paternal events.

2.3 The gamete-de-novo leg
--------------------------
A de novo segmental change introduced by the sperm is detectable as a segmental event whose
origin resolves to the father, and it is distinguishable from a post-zygotic segmental change
ONLY by uniformity across cells, not by genotype. Re-biopsy uniformity is the only channel
measured to break this tie (meiotic 64/64 uniform, mitotic 6/7 non-uniform, n=71). Your tool
cannot ask for a second biopsy, but it CAN ask for a file the user already has: if two units
of the same embryo were arrayed, the uniformity test is free. This is the single highest-value
addition to the mechanism side and it costs nothing at runtime.

2.4 Deletions, meiotic vs mitotic
---------------------------------
Weaker than for gains, and one specific confound is fatal. Monosomy is called by complete
absence of a parental haplotype, so a chromosome absent only from the SAMPLED CELLS reads as
monosomy: the meiotic/mitotic call for LOSSES is confounded by biopsy sampling, independent
of any statistic. The untransmitted-haplotype channel does help with a different question -
it identifies WHICH of the loaded parent's two homologues went missing rather than only which
parent - and that is exactly what distinguishes a meiotic loss of a specific homologue from a
mitotic loss. But it does not resolve the sampling confound. Report loss mechanism as
unresolved unless a second unit of the same embryo is loaded.

4. CALLING MATERNAL BY THE ABSENCE OF PATERNAL
=============================================
You asked for this to be treated rigorously rather than as a convenience. It cannot be, in
the form stated, and here is the precise reason.

4.1 There is no negative inference in the dosage channel
--------------------------------------------------------
"His contribution is intact while a change is present, so the change is hers" is not what the
allelic statistic measures. The statistic is a SIGNED displacement of the parent-allele share:
a deficit of his allele and an excess of his allele are both positive evidence, in opposite
directions, from the same markers. There is no "absent" reading in the sense of a missing
signal - the maternal case is the negative-going arm of the same measurement.

But your expectation of asymmetry is CORRECT, and I under-stated it in an earlier draft of
this section. Measured accuracy at k=400, f=0.10, calibrated posterior, split by which
parent's copy was actually affected ("other" = the un-genotyped parent, i.e. the
maternal-by-absence case):

  material        class     change is HERS   change is HIS   asymmetry
  TE              loss           0.777           0.854         +0.077
                  CNN-LOH        0.688           0.746         +0.058
                  gain           0.402           0.727         +0.325
  blastomere      loss           0.769           0.846         +0.077
                  CNN-LOH        0.602           0.685         +0.083
                  gain           0.438           0.602         +0.165
  bulk            loss           0.969           0.965         -0.004
                  CNN-LOH        0.629           0.677         +0.048
                  gain           0.835           0.860         +0.025
  single ESC WGA  loss           0.908           0.940         +0.031
                  CNN-LOH        0.615           0.579         -0.035
                  gain           0.615           0.677         +0.062

So: on BULK material the asymmetry is negligible (-0.004 to +0.048) and maternal-by-absence
is as good as paternal-by-presence. On amplified few-cell material it is real and it is
worst exactly where the sign inversion lives: a maternal-origin GAIN on TE material is called
correctly 40.2% of the time - below chance - against 72.7% for the paternal-origin gain, a
32.5-point gap. The mechanism is that the loaded parent's markers anchor the frame, so the
arm that requires displacement AWAY from the anchor is the one that loses when the class is
also uncertain.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE PRODUCT: maternal-by-absence and paternal-by-presence must be calibrated
SEPARATELY on amplified material. One pooled map over both arms will be over-confident on the
maternal arm and under-confident on the paternal arm, on TE and blastomere, for gains
especially. This is the same structural point as 1.3: the calibration has to be stratified on
anything that shifts the error rate, and the direction of the inference is one such thing.

4.2 Where a genuine negative inference does live, and there it IS asymmetric
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
The obligate-het channel is different. It asks whether a B allele appeared where the
homozygous-AA parent had no B to give. That IS a one-sided test and it has the ceiling you
suspected: failing to see the forbidden allele is consistent with (i) she did not transmit it,
(ii) dropout removed it, (iii) the window had too few informative markers. I measured the
boundary that governs (ii): on a real high-call-rate array the AA/AB genotype decision
boundary sits at BAF ~0.19, and the pooled het BAF (1-f)/(2-f) does not cross it until
f = 0.762. So the genotype channel is structurally blind below roughly f=0.76, which is
consistent with your audit's het-depletion detection floor of 0.65-0.90 arriving from a
different direction.

CEILING, AND IT SHOULD BE STRUCTURAL: a call resting on the obligate-het channel alone must
never reach band A. Cap it at B. The reason is not power, it is that the failure mode
(dropout) is the same event as the observation, so no amount of data separates them.

4.3 The untransmitted-haplotype channel does change this
-------------------------------------------------------
Yes, and it is the strongest thing you are not using. At markers where the loaded parent is
HETEROZYGOUS and the sample homozygous, the transmission is determined, so it reports WHICH of
his two homologues was transmitted. That makes his involvement positively excludable rather
than merely undetected, which is a different inferential object from 4.2. Your audit already
measures it as the only channel giving a single blastomere a defined floor at all (f80 0.628)
and 1.40-1.98x SNR. It should be the primary channel on blastomere material, not an addition.

4.4 What confounds a false "intact" reading
-------------------------------------------
  - clustered dropout: 1% of clean windows read 40% het-depleted with no event present
  - too few informative markers: at k=100 the gain power is 0.526, at k=25 it is 0.110
  - unseen WGA chemistry: the in-sample estimator errs OPTIMISTICALLY on amplified material,
    by 2.3x on an unseen chemistry. Multiply and state the widened floor.
  - self-phasing above f=0.5: a large loss removes the het calls that define the phase set,
    so the sample can no longer phase itself and the wrong-parent rate rises to 0.681
  - array with no self-reference: >30% deviant autosomes or call rate <0.70. Never call.

4.5 The one place the band structure is NOT honest, and I could not fix it with a gate
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Report this as a known defect rather than shipping over it.

Amplified material, true GAIN at f<=0.15, un-genotyped parent's copy affected, rows that
nonetheless reach band A or B: measured accuracy 0/34 on TE and 0/18 on blastomere. Zero.
Wilson 95% intervals [0.000, 0.102] and [0.000, 0.176]; one-sided zero-event bounds put
accuracy at <=0.084 and <=0.153. These rows are not weakly wrong, they are reliably inverted,
and the pooled band statistics hide them because the cell is small (430 rows of 207,360).

I tried to gate them out on observables alone and could not do it cleanly. The best
narrow gate (amplified, intensity direction unresolved at 99%, implied-gain f<0.20) catches
48% of the cell, touches 9.7% of amplified band A/B rows, and moves band A/B accuracy only
0.9747 -> 0.9779. Pushing to catch 63% costs a third of top-band coverage (42.0% -> 29.8% of
amplified rows). Search over 9 threshold combinations, all shown in direction_decidability.csv
and reproducible from calib_run.py.

Why no gate works: the rows are inverted precisely BECAUSE the observables cannot tell a small
gain from a small loss, so no function of those observables separates them. This is a real
identifiability limit, not a tuning failure.

THE REMEDY IS THE SECOND PARENT, and it is decisive rather than incremental. With both parents
the class is categorical rather than a threshold call: a gain shows a third parental
haplotype, a loss shows one missing, CNN-LOH shows two copies of one. Giving the posterior the
resolved class removes the below-chance behaviour everywhere. Measured wrong-parent rate,
k=400, class marginal -> class resolved:

  material        class     f=0.05         f=0.10         f=0.15         f=0.20
  TE              gain      0.565 -> 0.296  0.435 -> 0.148  0.298 -> 0.079  0.203 -> 0.041
                  CNN-LOH   0.320 -> 0.133  0.283 -> 0.025  0.331 -> 0.011  0.366 -> 0.008
  blastomere      gain      0.551 -> 0.288  0.480 -> 0.151  0.398 -> 0.068  0.265 -> 0.030
                  CNN-LOH   0.350 -> 0.166  0.356 -> 0.035  0.389 -> 0.011  0.425 -> 0.006
  single ESC WGA  gain      0.561 -> 0.188  0.354 -> 0.057  0.193 -> 0.014  0.120 -> 0.000
                  CNN-LOH   0.292 -> 0.053  0.403 -> 0.004  0.443 -> 0.000  0.349 -> 0.000

Not one cell remains at or above chance. Your audit's framing of the second parent as a 3.36x
floor improvement understates it: the floor ratio is the marker-count effect, and this is a
separate, larger, structural effect on the class-inversion failure. Design the product around
asking for the second parent, and say in the UI that a one-parent run cannot state a gain
origin at top confidence on amplified material.


5. THE LOCATION SIDE
====================

5.1 Honest breakpoint resolution
--------------------------------
Measured on a real high-call-rate array in your corpus, inter-marker spacing:

  all markers        median 1,737 bp    p90 8,380 bp     p99 21,166 bp
  informative (het)  median 8,683 bp    p90 46,553 bp    p99 159,948 bp

So a breakpoint localises to roughly 1.7 kb where all markers can be used (an intensity-based
edge) and to roughly 8.7 kb where only informative markers count (an allelic edge), with a
long tail: 1 in 100 informative gaps exceeds 160 kb. Report the flanking informative marker
positions as the interval, not a point.

5.2 The 50-100x claim, restated properly
----------------------------------------
"Origin uncertainty dominates breakpoint uncertainty by 50-100x" is not a comparison of like
things - one is a probability, the other a distance. The defensible version: at k=400 the
median event span is 47.6 Mb and the breakpoint interval is ~8.7 kb, so the breakpoint is
0.018% of the event extent. Meanwhile the origin call at that same k carries a wrong-parent
rate of 4.5-20% on amplified material. Both statements are in comparable terms now:
positional error is negligible relative to the event; assignment error is not.

5.3 Does breakpoint error propagate into the origin call
-------------------------------------------------------
Only through marker count, and weakly. A misplaced boundary adds or removes markers at the
edge; k explains 2.4% of confidence variance against f's 51.7%. A boundary error would have to
move enough markers to change k by a factor of about 2 before it moved the band. At 8.7 kb
resolution on a 47.6 Mb event, it cannot. The exception your notes already identify is
heterodisomic UPD, where the segment structure IS the finding and the boundary is the result
rather than a nuisance.

5.4 What interval to report for a whole-chromosome event
-------------------------------------------------------
Report the assayed extent, explicitly labelled as such: first to last passing marker on the
chromosome, with the chromosome length alongside. "chr7:193,847-159,119,486 (assayed extent;
chr7 is 159,138,663 bp in GRCh37)". This neither overstates precision (you did not measure the
telomeres) nor understates it (the marker span is what you actually interrogated). Do not
report the centromere gap as covered - it is not; median 74 markers within +/-0.5 Mb and the
UCSC hg19 gap itself is 3.0 Mb of unassayable sequence on chr1, 10, 11, 12 and others.


6. WHAT YOU CANNOT DO, NAMED SO YOU DO NOT CHASE IT
===================================================

Not separable on WGA few-cell material at any confidence, with one parent:
  - tandem vs inserted duplication. No positional information exists in either channel.
  - MII-without-recombination vs post-zygotic mitotic. Not separable by anyone, on genotype
    alone, at any material quality. Three independent sources acknowledge the collapse.
  - whole-chromosome heterodisomic UPD from the embryo alone. Needs a trio. 32% of known UPD
    cases show no significant homozygous stretch at all.
  - copy-number CLASS on TE and blastomere at k<=800. Your own audit: 89-100% unresolved,
    window log2R sd 0.17-0.22 against a needed 0.029-0.081. This consult adds why it matters:
    the unresolved class is what inverts the origin call.
  - gain origin at top confidence, amplified material, f<0.15, maternal arm (section 4.5).
  - any origin call on an array with >30% deviant autosomes or call rate <0.70. No
    self-reference exists. All 70 whole-chromosome losses in your series sit here.
  - any origin call with zero parents loaded. Your measured false-origin rate goes from 0.0790
    with a real second parent to 1.0000 from a population median.

Where "always call" must become "always report, sometimes without a parent":
  narrower than I expected, and narrower than the current tool assumes. It is NOT the whole
  D band - D is calibrated within itself (section 1.4) and can carry its weak number
  honestly. It is exactly two situations:
    - the inverted-gain cell of 4.5 (amplified material, implied-gain f<0.15, direction
      unresolved). Here the error is systematic, not weak, so a number would be a lie.
      Withhold the parent, state "class-inverted risk: gain origin not stated without a
      second parental array".
    - the structural refusals: no parent loaded, or an array with no self-reference
      (>30% deviant autosomes or call rate <0.70). No statistic exists to calibrate.
  Everywhere else, report the event, its interval, its class if resolved, and the calibrated
  number with its band, plus the decomposition from 1.5.

Two parents versus one, since you will make this decision deliberately:
  the gap is large and it is larger than your notes say. Floor ratio 3.36x on TE, plus the
  class-inversion fix in 4.5 which is a categorical improvement rather than a ratio, plus
  heterodisomic UPD becoming visible at all, plus MI/MII staging becoming possible in
  principle (though still 20-25% inconclusive). If one product decision follows from this
  consult, it is to treat the second parental array as the primary ask.


7. HOW TO DEMONSTRATE CALIBRATION, GIVEN THE TRUTH SET YOU HAVE
===============================================================

7.1 Your truth set is smaller than it looks
-------------------------------------------
The haploid pronuclei are 330 chromosome-level tests but only 15 independent units, and the
clustering is severe: per-unit accuracy ranges 0.682-1.000, between-unit variance 0.0095,
design effect approximately 3.9 in variance. Consequences:

  naive Wilson on 330 chromosomes      0.9455 [0.9154, 0.9652]
  cluster-robust on 15 units           0.9455 [0.8961, 0.9948]

and the gated result that reads best is the one to be most careful with:

  gated accuracy 1.0000 on 251/330 chromosomes, but those 251 come from 13 units. The
  honest zero-event bound is therefore error <= 0.206, NOT error <= 0.012. Quoting
  "1.0000 on 251" as evidence of >=99% accuracy overstates by a factor of 17.

Also note the truth set is not balanced across the thing being tested: maternal units are
154/154 correct and paternal 158/176 (0.8977). Any calibration built on it inherits that.

7.2 What a reliability bin actually needs
-----------------------------------------
Two-sided 95% Wilson half-width no wider than half the gap to the adjacent band:

  band centred 0.99, gap 0.04  ->  134 independent events per bin
  band centred 0.95, gap 0.10  ->   82
  band centred 0.85, gap 0.15  ->   87
  band centred 0.70, gap 0.20  ->   78

Multiply by the design effect (~3.9) for clustered rows: roughly 300-520 clustered events per
bin, or 4 bins x ~100 independent units. You do not have that in real events and will not
soon. This is the honest reason the injection experiment is not optional.

7.3 The minimum publishable calibration claim
---------------------------------------------
Three legs, and you can do all three now:

  1. Real-noise injection with leave-one-array-out recalibration, which is what this consult
     did: 276,480 injections, 14 arrays, 4 material classes, ECE 0.0027-0.0072. The claim this
     supports is "calibrated with respect to real array noise, dropout and within-array drift,
     with the event injected". State that limitation explicitly - it does not establish
     calibration against real biological events.
  2. Real-event anchor on the 15 haploid pronuclei with CLUSTER-ROBUST intervals, reported as
     15 units not 330 chromosomes. This is the leg that connects to biology, and it is weak;
     say so.
  3. Replicate-array concordance as a consistency check: the same DNA arrayed twice should
     land in the same band. You have replicate arrays; this costs nothing and it tests the
     one thing injections cannot, namely whether array identity (8.9% of confidence variance)
     is being absorbed correctly.

Do NOT claim calibration against real UPD or real mosaics of known fraction. You have neither,
and the literature check found no published reliability diagram in PGT at all - so there is no
external benchmark to borrow either. That absence is itself worth stating in a paper: it makes
the calibration the contribution rather than a methods detail.

7.4 The honest limitation on the whole exercise
-----------------------------------------------
The injections carry real noise but the event is synthetic and, critically, the informative
set was defined from parental genotypes with the measured truly-homozygous dilution imported
(0.099 bulk, 0.202 single ESC, 0.683 TE, 0.468 blastomere from your own window-composition
table). That is the honest deployment frame and it is why these floors are worse than a
naive construction would give. What it still does not capture: correlated dropout structure
specific to a real event, chemistry not in your calibration set (expect a 2.3x optimistic
error there), and biological states like consanguinity that mimic the signal.


8. WHAT I WOULD BUILD, IN ORDER
===============================
1. Marginalise the class into the origin posterior. This is the correctness fix, not an
   improvement: it removes a systematic below-chance failure. Everything else is secondary.
2. Per-class isotonic recalibration, fitted leave-one-array-out, one map per material class
   and per resolved class. Ship the maps as data, not as thresholds in code.
3. Four bands. D keeps its number (it is calibrated to within 1.2 points inside itself) but
   carries "not suitable for a reporting decision" and the four-way decomposition. The
   number is withheld only in the 4.5 inverted-gain cell and the structural refusals.
4. Retire the f=0.30 blanket withhold. It mislabels 24% of blastomere events that are in
   band A and it lets through the low-f gain inversions that are the actual danger.
5. Make the second parental array the primary ask in the UI, with the specific reason:
   without it, a gain origin cannot be stated at top confidence on amplified material.
6. Add the same-embryo-second-unit test, which is free at runtime and is the only channel
   measured to separate meiotic from mitotic.
7. Ship BPH-positive / "CN3, not BPH" and drop MI/MII staging.
8. Report the assayed marker extent for whole-chromosome events, and flanking informative
   marker positions for segmental breakpoints.
