ORIGINMARKER 4.1 - AUDIT
========================
One record for the parental-origin work. The per-topic files remain for detail:
AUDIT.txt, BREAKPOINTS-AUDIT.txt, PROGENITOR-AUDIT.txt, FORMATS.txt,
regions/FINDINGS.txt, gse19247/README.txt.

Everything below is measured. Where a claim could not be tested it says so.


WHAT IS VALIDATED
-----------------
- Gain DETECTION on real trisomies. 330 karyotype-confirmed trisomy 21 single cells, 100 of them
  re-karyotyped blind by an independent laboratory. AUC 0.9779 against 114 confirmed euploid on
  one platform, 0.9991 against 27 on the other. Inside the trisomy cells chr21 ranks first of 22
  autosomes on BOTH platforms, which is what shows the statistic reads dosage and not amplification.
- Breakpoint refinement. Median 9 markers, about 60 kb, with an interval on every edge. 12.2x
  better than the window edge on public data. Needs no parental genotypes.
- Sub-chromosome parental resolution, AGAINST A BULK PARENT. Per 5-Mb bin, AUC 0.9301 and
  sensitivity 0.8148 at 95% specificity, measured on a bulk-genotyped father's biparental children
  against his own one-genome products.
- Orientation. 78,227 of 78,227 real markers invariant, and gain and loss now pinned in opposite
  directions so reusing one call for the other event fails a test rather than naming the wrong
  parent.
- Format handling. Three deposit shapes in circulation, all sharing the AX- probeset namespace and
  joining directly: tab-separated .CEL.probes; comma-separated GEO .CEL.txt, which omits log2R and
  supplies normalized_intensity instead; and the repo's subset. Pinned against their real headers.


WHAT IS NOT
-----------
- A parental SPLIT. No number of the form "x% of losses are maternal" is admissible from this
  material yet. Six one-directional results were produced during this work and every one was
  artefact; the reasons are listed below.
- Gain ORIGIN on a confirmed trisomy. Detection is validated on 330 of them; naming the parent of
  one is not, because no confirmed trisomy with a genotyped parent has been found.
- Anything at 5-Mb resolution against a RECONSTRUCTED parent. That configuration measures 0.065 to
  0.184 sensitivity, and it is the configuration most of this series is in.


THE CONFOUNDS, EACH MEASURED
----------------------------
- A self-derived null turns platform failure into a parental result. 154 regions, all maternal,
  with chr16:0-1Mb appearing in 18 of 32 cells. An external per-window null cut it to 59.
- A single-parent reference cannot see a paternal loss. Headroom +0.019 above the centre against
  -0.090 below, on a 0.10 threshold, so one direction is invisible by geometry.
- A median B-allele frequency measures dropout, not dosage. Only 21-53% of obligate-het markers
  are called heterozygous; the rest sit at zero and drag the median. No threshold recovers it.
  Genotype calls do work and are what the shipped module uses.
- Reconstructing a father from the cells being scored is circular unless the criterion is
  HETEROZYGOSITY in the child and the selecting cells are held out. The wrong criterion put
  paternal share at 0.96-0.98 where 0.5 is correct.
- Relatedness baselines do not transfer between platforms. Unrelated adults sit near 0.30 on a
  dense Illumina panel and at 0.0727 on GPL28377, which is enriched for low-frequency variants.
  A cutoff carried across admits unrelated material, and did.
- Sampling is not searching. A 90-array sample concluded no biparental child of the bulk father
  existed; scanning all 884 found nine children across four experiments, five biparental. Two
  conclusions in this audit were wrong for that reason and are corrected here.


THE CALIBRATION, AND A CORRECTION TO THIS AUDIT'S OWN EXPLANATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
A 29-region result, all maternal and none paternal at p < 0.0001, was produced on five biparental
children of a genotyped father and is NOT reportable. The control that refutes it: the
non-paternal allele fraction in regions with no loss (0.007 to 0.042) matches the loss regions
(0.005 to 0.043) and is slightly lower where a copy is supposedly missing, so the statistic
assigning the parent carries no information about the parent.

This audit previously attributed that to the array's per-allele calling asymmetry. MEASURED, IT IS
NOT THE CAUSE. At a marker where the father is heterozygous, meiosis gives each of his gametes A
or B with probability exactly one half, so his haploid products calibrate the array against a known
truth with no biology in it. Over 127,826 such markers and four of his gametes:

    AA fraction 0.4948, 0.4945, 0.4880, 0.4964      mean 0.4934 against an expected 0.5000
    bias -0.0066; the array calls A 0.974x as often as B when the truth is 1:1

Correcting an observed non-paternal fraction of 0.02 gives 0.0195. The bias is real, it is under
one percent, and it explains none of the excess.

The actual explanation is simpler and was missed. At a father-AA marker most homozygous calls are
legitimately AA because the mother transmitted A as well, so a low non-paternal fraction is the
EXPECTED baseline rather than evidence of anything. The bins flagged as losses were amplification
dropout rather than copy loss, which is why they carried no parental signal: nothing had been lost.

  So the calibration is done, the array is close to unbiased, and the maternal excess was a
  detection artefact of the loss caller rather than of the platform.


WHAT WOULD CLOSE THE REMAINING GAP
-----------------------------------
- A bulk parental array per experimental group. This is the whole of it. With one, resolution goes
  from 0.065-0.184 sensitivity to 0.815 at 95% specificity. The laboratory is isolating sperm, and
  a bulk sperm donor already exists for part of the set.
- A loss caller that separates copy loss from amplification dropout, since the current one does
  not and that is what produced the 29 unsupported regions.
- Parity confirmed on material where parity is known, before any split is reported. The
  non-allele-specific Cas9 arm is the candidate, conditional on the guides being confirmed
  non-allele-specific.
- Roughly 20-30 oocyte donors before an asymmetry claim carries meaning. At 10 the minimum
  detectable effect is a paternal fraction of 0.64 to 0.70.


DATA
----
- 884 probe exports held locally, verified byte-for-byte against source, 736 unique samples.
- 684 CEL files named in the three submission sheets; 474 present, 210 absent from the shared
  drive and never deposited there.
- One cumulus sample in the entire set, 14801-05, bulk-quality and confirmed as the mother of the
  other arrays in its experiment.
- 14 polar bodies, publicly deposited, genome-wide heterozygosity median 0.1116 where truth is
  zero. That is the error floor for one-genome material on this platform.
- Two arrays labelled MII oocyte biopsies read inside the biparental range and should not be used
  as maternal references.


PARENTAL LINKAGE: WHAT HAS BEEN TESTED, AND THE PROOF OF CONCEPT THAT DOES NOT EXIST PUBLICLY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Validated, on data with known relationships:
- Parent-child versus unrelated separation. Three tiers on real single cells: same individual
  0.097-0.119 opposite-homozygote, related 0.14-0.18, unrelated 0.326-0.371. The tiers do not
  overlap.
- A maternal reference confirmed against 33 arrays, with one of the donor's own MII oocytes at
  0.0061 as an internal control, and four amplification failures separating at 0.31-0.52.
- Parental direction at 5 Mb against a BULK parent: AUC 0.9301, sensitivity 0.8148 at 95%
  specificity, on a bulk-genotyped father's biparental children against his own one-genome
  products.
- Platform allele-calling bias against meiosis, where the true ratio is fixed at one half by
  segregation: 0.4934 over 127,826 markers and four gametes, a bias of -0.0066.
- Trisomy DETECTION on 330 karyotype-confirmed trisomy 21 cells, AUC 0.9779 and 0.9991.

NOT VALIDATED, AND NOT VALIDATABLE ON PUBLIC DATA AS OF THIS AUDIT: the parental ORIGIN of a
confirmed trisomy. That requires an aneuploid sample with BOTH parents genotyped on an array.

Searched twice, systematically, and it does not exist in GEO:
- Trisomy series with parents: GSE19247 carries 330 confirmed trisomy 21 cells and NO bulk DNA at
  all, so its parents are single amplified buccal cells rather than parental gDNA.
- Trisomy-with-family series: GSE230995 and GSE230994 are RNA-seq and GRO-seq of trisomy 21
  families, so they carry expression rather than genotypes and cannot answer a parental-origin
  question.
- The one design that fits, 1,270 trophectoderm biopsies with both parents genotyped and 366
  meiotic aneuploidies, is Gennet's and is not public: patient confidentiality prevents sharing
  the SNP files, and access is by request to the senior author with local ethics approval.

So the honest position is that trisomy DETECTION is validated at scale on real confirmed cases and
trisomy ORIGIN is not, because the material to validate it on is not publicly available. The
nearest substitute already run is the constructed positive control, which re-weights real allele
intensities on a real array and recovers both directions, and it is labelled constructed wherever
it is quoted.


THE DROPOUT BLOCKER, RESOLVED: THE SHIPPED CALLER ALREADY REQUIRES INTENSITY
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
This audit named "a loss caller that separates copy loss from amplification dropout" as the
remaining blocker on a parental split. Checked against the code and the data, the SHIPPED caller
already does this and the failure was in an offline script, not in the product.

The discriminator is physical. Amplification dropout removes genotype CALLS but not DNA, so
intensity is unchanged. A real copy loss removes both. The two channels therefore disagree on
dropout and agree on a real loss.

The shipped path takes losses only from the intensity channel: whole-chromosome losses from the
call-rate collapse, and segmental ones from scanCopyNumber, which reads log2R against a
per-chromosome external null. A region flagged by heterozygosity alone never reaches result.losses.

The 29 unsupported regions came from an offline het-only script that had no such gate. Measured,
the separation is complete:

  the 5 cells carrying all 29 het-based "losses"     0, 0, 0, 0 and 1 intensity-based regions
  cells with a genuine intensity-based region        173 of 884

So the het channel flagged 29 losses in cells the intensity channel calls clean, and the intensity
channel is the one validated on 330 karyotype-confirmed trisomies at AUC 0.9779 and 0.9991.

  CONSEQUENCE. The blocker on a parental split is NOT the loss caller. It is the parental
  reference: a bulk parental array per experimental group. Any future analysis must take its
  regions from result.segments and result.chroms rather than re-deriving them from heterozygosity,
  which is what produced every one of the artefacts recorded above.


THE ARRAYS WITH A PARENT AND THE ARRAYS WITH AN EVENT ARE DISJOINT
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Run through the shipped modules (audit/linkage.ts) across all 884 arrays, four experiments:

  83   arrays meet the bulk-reference bar (call >= 93%, heterozygosity 12-22%)
  20   arrays are CHILDREN of one of them, established in two stages
  217  arrays are close relatives that are NOT children: same individual, clonal cells or haploid
       products, each recorded with the fraction that excluded it
  192  arrays carry a copy-number region
   0   arrays are in BOTH sets

That last line is the result. Every one of the 20 children was checked against the region tables
independently and carries no copy-number event; the 192 arrays that do carry one have no genotyped
parent. The two populations do not intersect anywhere in this series.

So the parental question cannot be answered from this material, and the reason is not the method.
Sub-chromosome parental direction measures AUC 0.9301 against a bulk parent, and detection measures
AUC 0.9779 on 330 karyotype-confirmed trisomies. Both work. There is simply no array here that is
both a child of a genotyped parent AND carries an event to assign.

A SELECTION EFFECT IS THE LIKELY CAUSE AND IT IS NOT REASSURING. To be counted a child, an array
needs a bulk-quality reference in its own experiment. Experiments with bulk-quality material are
the better-run ones, and better-run material carries fewer events. So the requirement that makes a
parental call possible is correlated with the absence of the thing to be called.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY. It is not evidence that events are rare, nor that parental origin is
unknowable in this system. It says the 884 arrays in hand cannot answer it, and that adding a bulk
parental array to an experiment that ALREADY has events is what would change that. A parent
collected alongside the material that carries events, rather than in place of it.

TWO STAGES, BECAUSE ONE IS NOT ENOUGH. Opposite-homozygote rate alone called 13 of 85 arrays
children in one experiment and 10 were wrong: the rate is near zero for a parent and child AND for
the same genome twice. Requiring the candidate to read BIPARENTAL against the reference removed
them. The 217 set-aside arrays are the visible evidence that the second stage does work.


PARENTAL ORIGIN WITHOUT A PARENT: THE SIBLING-REFERENCED CALL (4.6.0)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Two findings from an external assessment overturn what this audit previously recorded as limits,
and the second removes the blocker entirely.

THE ONE-PARENT ASYMMETRY WAS A CENTRING ARTEFACT, NOT A GEOMETRIC LIMIT. The two loss hypotheses
sit symmetrically about the disomic expectation at 1-q and 1, so headroom is q/2 on each side. For
every q below 0.5 the majority of reference-homozygous markers sit exactly at share 1.0, which
makes the MEDIAN the ceiling and the upward headroom 0.000 by construction: 91.6% of markers at 1.0
for q=0.10, 78.8% for q=0.25, 66.1% for q=0.40. A statistic whose centre is a boundary point of its
own support can only move one way. That, not dropout, generated every one-directional result
recorded above. Dropout is mean-unbiased: expected share 0.8754 to 0.8748 across ADO 0.000 to
0.600 against an analytic 0.8750.

THE DISJOINTNESS DOES NOT BLOCK ANYTHING. An anchor names all four founder haplotypes for every
embryo in its donor group, and the 2PN groups have ONE egg donor each. So one array per donor group
suffices and the anchor never has to share an array with the event. The 0-of-192 intersection
recorded above is real and irrelevant.

WHAT SHIPS IN 4.6.0. web/src/siblingOrigin.ts calls which parental copy is missing using the
embryo's OWN event-free cells as the reference, with no parental array at any point.

  Heterozygosity is established from SIBLINGS, never from the cell being scored. Conditioning on
  the scored cell's own heterozygous calls selects the markers where both alleles survived and
  forces the share to 0.5 at every one of them, leaving nothing to read.
  There is no centre. Each marker contributes a likelihood under three hypotheses and the region's
  evidence is their product, so the quantity that was mis-centred does not exist here.
  The third hypothesis is no-deletion, which absorbs dropout clusters. Verified: a region of
  balanced homozygotes with no heterozygotes reads as no deletion rather than as a lost copy, and
  reads the same with the alleles swapped.

DROP-IN, MEASURED HERE, AND IT IS BETTER THAN ASSUMED. False heterozygosity is the only
one-directional term, because a falsely heterozygous marker always retains the reference allele.
Measured on 113 same-genome array pairs across four experiments, being replicate and clonal
material where a marker the reference calls homozygous is homozygous in truth:

    ROBLES  n=40  median 0.0525        DIETER  n=25  median 0.0435
    JENNA   n=16  median 0.0410        TREFF   n=32  median 0.0390

The assessment assumed 0.0854 from 7 arrays. Every experiment here sits below it.

THE SIBLING RULE MUST SCALE, and at the assumed drop-in a fixed rule crossed the wall. Expected
false-het fraction by panel size, at the measured 0.0435:

    siblings   agree>=   phi        a fixed >=2 rule would give
    2          2         0.0019     0.0019
    4          2         0.0107     0.0107
    5          3         0.0008     0.0173
    8          4         0.0002     0.0445
    10         5         0.0000     0.0675

Under a fixed rule phi RISES with panel size, because more cells give a spurious call more chances
to appear, so adding arrays would worsen the bias while appearing to improve reliability. Under the
majority rule it falls. At the measured drop-in no panel size approaches the 0.15 wall.

WHAT IS NOT DONE. The module is not wired into Syngamy, which streams one sample at a time and
would need a two-pass restructure to hold a sibling panel. The science route is the audit harness,
which already reads many arrays at once. Stated rather than left to be discovered.
