REGION CALLING AGAINST A SINGLE MATERNAL REFERENCE: TWO CONFOUNDS, BOTH MEASURED
================================================================================
Material: experiment 14801, the one complete unit in the laboratory set. 825,657-marker arrays.
Reference: 14801-05, the single cumulus sample, maternal somatic DNA at bulk quality on this
platform (call 0.950, het 0.145 against 0.954 / 0.168 for known bulk gDNA).

THE REFERENCE IS CONFIRMED, NOT ASSUMED
----------------------------------------
Opposite-homozygote rate of every array in the experiment against the cumulus donor:

  14801-02   0.0012      33 arrays span 0.0012 to 0.114
  14801-12   0.0061      an MII oocyte biopsy, her own gamete, which is the internal control
  ...
  14801-20/23/27/38   0.307 to 0.523   amplification failures, excluded

Unrelated adults sit near 0.30. Everything under 0.12 is her material or her child. So the
maternal assignment is measured, and the four failures separate cleanly from it.

CONFOUND 1: A SELF-DERIVED NULL CONVERTS PLATFORM ARTEFACT INTO A PARENTAL RESULT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scoring each cell against its own genome-wide centre returns 154 regions, and the shape of that
result is the giveaway rather than the count:

  154 of 154 called MATERNAL copy missing.  Zero paternal.
  60% fall in 1-Mb bins recurring in 5 or more different cells.
  chr16:0-1Mb appears in 18 of 32 cells.  chr6:32-33Mb, the HLA region, in 10.
  chr11:2-3Mb, an imprinted cluster, in 7.  chr1:2-3Mb and chr19:0-1Mb, GC-rich subtelomeres, in 5.

No biological process runs one way in every cell and lands on the same megabase in half of them.
These are places where genotyping fails on this platform, and the failure drops maternal-matching
signal, so a self-derived null reports it as a parental finding.

Replacing the null with the SAME WINDOW ACROSS THE OTHER CELLS, each cell held out of its own
null, removes them: 154 regions fall to 59, the cohort median at the surviving positions sits at
about 0.000, and what remains is cell-specific at 5 to 23 robust SD. That is the repo's existing
external-null principle applied at region level, and it was necessary here.

CONFOUND 2: THE STATISTIC CANNOT SEE A PATERNAL LOSS AT ALL
------------------------------------------------------------
After the external null all 59 surviving regions are STILL maternal, and that is not biology
either. Measured, pooled over every window and cell:

  headroom ABOVE each cell's centre   p99  +0.0191
  headroom BELOW                      p1   -0.0904
                                           -------------
  4.7x more range downward, and the detection threshold is 0.10

A lost PATERNAL copy pushes maternal share UP, toward a ceiling 0.019 away. A lost MATERNAL copy
pushes it DOWN, and the observed events reach -0.45. So one direction is structurally invisible
and the other is easy, and any parental split computed this way is a property of the geometry.

The cause is the informative set. With only the mother genotyped, most of her homozygous markers
are uninformative because the father carried the same allele, and those markers sit at share 1.0
and pin the centre near the top of the range. The dynamic range above the centre is what is left
over, which is nothing.

  CONSEQUENCE. Do not report a parental split from a single-parent reference. An external review
  independently identified detection asymmetry as the binding confound for this question and put
  the exchange rate at a 7.7% maternal detection shortfall being enough to manufacture an apparent
  paternal fraction of 0.52. What is measured here is that same failure at its limit: a 100:0
  split produced entirely by measurement.

WHAT IS NEEDED, AND IT IS ALREADY INDICATED
--------------------------------------------
Restrict to markers where the father DIFFERS from the mother, which is the obligate-het class the
gain and loss callers already consume. That needs the father. He is recoverable: cross-donor
sharing reconstructs a father at 99.28% over 65.6% of markers from 8 or more embryos spanning 3 or
more oocyte donors, and unlike the maternal case that reconstruction does not bias the direction
under test. Where the EYS sperm donor was the source, his bulk array exists and no inference is
needed at all.

The 59 regions are therefore kept as a MATERNAL-LOSS CANDIDATE LIST with breakpoint intervals, not
as a parental split, and the strongest of them are worth checking against the laboratory's manual
breakpoint maps:

  14801-29  chr9   21.22-22.99, 34.47-35.75, 106.93-108.13 Mb   z -17 to -23
  14801-30  chr12  32.48-34.01, 111.32-113.35, 120.47-122.22 Mb  z -14 to -19
  14801-19  chr5  178.79-180.17 Mb                                z -21


CONFOUND 3: RECONSTRUCTING THE FATHER FROM THE CELLS THAT WILL BE SCORED IS CIRCULAR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The father was called homozygous-opposite at a maternal-homozygous marker when a large majority of
her children carried a NON-MATERNAL allele there. That criterion counts a child reading the other
homozygote, which is a maternal copy ABSENT, as evidence about the father. It therefore selects
markers where the maternal allele is missing and then measures that it is missing.

Measured: 11,600 markers selected this way, and paternal share at them sits at a centre of 0.96 to
0.98 in 27 of 33 cells. A biparental cell at a true obligate-het marker reads about 0.5. The
selection produced the answer.

  Correct criterion: the child must be HETEROZYGOUS at the marker, not merely non-maternal. Only
  AB is evidence that the father contributed the opposite allele to a mother who could not.
  Selection must also come from cells DISJOINT from the ones being scored, or the same circularity
  returns in a weaker form.

THE ORIENTATION ITSELF IS CORRECT, and three cells prove it independently of the above. Their
centres sit where the biology says they should, without being told what they are:

  14801-04   paternal share 0.158   the POLAR BODY, maternal material, so near zero is right
  14801-02   0.104   14801-06  0.112     maternal-only material
  14801-01   0.547   14801-07  0.558     biparental
  14801-12   0.417   an MII oocyte biopsy

So the statistic separates maternal-only from biparental material on real arrays with no labels
supplied. What is wrong is the marker selection feeding it, not the measure.


CONFOUND 4: ON THIS MATERIAL A MEDIAN B-ALLELE FREQUENCY MEASURES DROPOUT, NOT DOSAGE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
With the father called leave-one-out from HETEROZYGOUS children only, the circularity of confound 3
is gone: centres move from 0.96-0.98 to a range that responds to the threshold, and the p1/p99
headroom becomes symmetric. Two things then failed to line up, and the second explains the first.

Paternal share never reaches the 0.5 a biparental cell must show, at any marker-selection
threshold, and the plateau rules out selection as the cause:

  HET_FRAC   markers   median centre
      0.30     78093          0.138
      0.45     20653          0.191
      0.60      4449          0.220
      0.65      2597          0.252

The cause is that the median is taken over markers most of which have dropped to a homozygote.
Measured per cell at HET_FRAC 0.50:

  markers CALLED heterozygous          0.21 to 0.53
  median BAF-share over all markers    0.04 to 0.25
  BAF-share AT the heterozygous calls  0.26 to 0.43

So BAF at a genuine heterozygous call is only mildly compressed, and the low medians come from the
50-80% of markers that are homozygous through allele dropout and sit at share 0. A median over
that mixture reports the dropout rate.

  CONSEQUENCE. The dosage statistic that works on bulk and on pronuclei does not transfer to WGA
  blastomeres, and no threshold rescues it. The genotype-based statistic does: count the fraction
  of obligate-het markers CALLED heterozygous, which is what obligateHet.ts already implements and
  is insensitive to where BAF lands inside a het call. Region calling on this material should be
  built on that fraction, with BAF used only as a secondary channel on samples whose call rate
  supports it.

ORIENTATION, RECORDED BECAUSE IT WAS GOT WRONG HERE FIRST
----------------------------------------------------------
At an obligate-het marker the father carries the allele the mother does not. Where she is AA his
share is the B-allele frequency itself; where she is BB it is the complement. Taking the maternal
orientation and labelling it paternal inverts every call downstream, silently, because the
distribution still looks reasonable. Both orientations are now pinned in gainOrigin.check.ts.


THE GENOTYPE STATISTIC WORKS, AND VALIDATES ON KNOWN MATERIAL
---------------------------------------------------------------
Rebuilt on the channel that survives WGA: per 5-Mb bin, the fraction of obligate-het markers
CALLED heterozygous, against the same bin in the other cells with each cell held out of its own
null. Direction comes from the non-heterozygous calls, by which parent's allele they carry.

  node audit/regions/geno_regions.py        9 regions across 33 cells

Every one of the 9 is PATERNAL copy missing, and they fall in exactly two cells:

  14801-02   8 regions   chr1, 7, 8, 11, 12, 18, 19, 20     his-allele fraction 0.00-0.01
  14801-16   1 region    chr1 155.00-159.99 Mb              his-allele fraction 0.22

Those two are the positive control, and neither was labelled to the caller. 14801-02 reads 0.0012
opposite-homozygote against the mother, which is her own genome rather than a child's, and
14801-16 is a POLAR BODY. Both are maternal-only material and therefore paternal-absent by
construction. The statistic found the right cells, the right direction, and a his-allele fraction
of essentially zero, from genotype calls alone.

Three earlier attempts on the same data returned 154 regions all maternal (self-null artefact),
59 all maternal (single-parent headroom), and 1 (BAF measuring dropout). This one returns 9, all
in cells that must carry the event.

SENSITIVITY IS THE OPEN NUMBER, NOT DIRECTION. A maternal-only cell is paternal-absent on every
bin, and 8 of about 560 were flagged in 14801-02, so the per-bin detection rate at z >= 4 is low
even where truth is certain. Bin size sets it: at 2 Mb a bin holds about 43 obligate-het markers
and the sampling standard deviation of the fraction is 0.076, the same size as the signal. 5 Mb
halves that. Larger bins or a segment-level likelihood, rather than independent bins, is the way
to raise it, and the positive control above is what any change should be measured against.

Nothing here is a parental asymmetry result. It is the first configuration on this material where
a parental call is made for a reason that survives its own controls.


THE RESOLUTION THIS MATERIAL SUPPORTS, MEASURED
------------------------------------------------
Truth for these numbers is material type from the submission sheet, not a call: polar bodies, MII
oocyte biopsies and isolated pronuclei carry one parental genome, so every scoreable window in them
is a true positive for a uniparental call.

  PER 5-Mb BIN, binomial likelihood ratio      sensitivity 0.065 at false-positive 0.027
                                               sensitivity 0.140 at false-positive 0.075
  PER CHROMOSOME, heterozygous fraction        AUC 0.922
  PER CELL, all autosomes pooled               AUC 0.804

Per-bin discrimination is roughly two-fold and that is not usable. The binomial is the wrong error
model: it assumes markers within a bin fail independently, and whole-genome amplification drops
them in spatially clustered runs, so the likelihood ratio overstates significance and returned 419
regions across nearly every cell. Chromosome scale is where this material actually separates.

  CONSEQUENCE FOR THE STUDY. A parental label can be attached at chromosome scale on WGA single
  cells with this marker set. It cannot be attached at 5 Mb. Breakpoint POSITION is a separate
  question and is refined from the dosage channel, which does not depend on parental genotypes;
  what is bounded here is how finely the PARENT can be named.

SUB-CHROMOSOME PARENTAL CALLING FAILS ON A RECONSTRUCTED FATHER. THREE METHODS, ONE ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sensitivity measured against material type, where every chromosome in a polar body, MII oocyte
biopsy or isolated pronucleus is a true positive:

  per 5-Mb bin, MAD z on the fraction     8 detections of ~560 in a cell uniparental everywhere
  per 5-Mb bin, binomial likelihood       sensitivity 0.065 at false-positive 0.027
  per segment, maximum-segment-sum with
  a within-cell permutation null          sensitivity 0.184 at false-positive 0.133

The third was built to fix the first two: it pools evidence across bins instead of testing them
independently, and its null is the cell's own bins reshuffled, so the marginal noise including
clustered dropout is preserved exactly and only spatial structure is destroyed. It still separates
barely 1.4-fold, and the segments it returns are 40 to 190 Mb, which is a whole chromosome by
another name.

Per chromosome the same underlying fraction reaches AUC 0.922. So the failure is specific to
sub-chromosome resolution and is not a threshold that was set wrong.

WHAT LIMITS IT. The obligate-het marker set, not the statistic. The father here is reconstructed
from the mother's own children at a 0.30 heterozygous-fraction cut, which admits markers where he
was HETEROZYGOUS - a normal cell reads those AB only half the time - and markers where the mother
was miscalled homozygous. Both dilute the class in the same direction, and no downstream test
recovers what the marker class does not carry. Raising the cut removes the contamination and the
markers together: 0.60 keeps 4,401 of 654,348, which is 8 per 5-Mb bin.

  THE FIX IS A GENOTYPED FATHER, and it is available rather than hypothetical. The EYS sperm donor
  is on this platform as bulk gDNA in four replicates where he was the sperm source, and the
  laboratory is isolating sperm for the rest. With a real paternal array the obligate-het class is
  defined rather than inferred, and every measurement above should be repeated on it before any
  conclusion about resolution is treated as final.

WHAT IS SAFE TO STATE NOW
--------------------------
  parental origin, whole chromosome    AUC 0.922, usable
  parental origin, segment             not supported on a reconstructed father
  breakpoint POSITION                  a separate question, answered by the dosage channel, which
                                       needs no parental genotypes and refines to about 60 kb


THE POLAR-BODY ERROR FLOOR, MEASURED ON 14 REAL ARRAYS
--------------------------------------------------------
An external review had to INTERPOLATE the error model for a polar body between measured 1C and bulk
rates, because the public series it worked from contains none. GSE186407 contains 14, from 6 oocyte
donors, and they are deposited as ingestible probe exports.

  genome-wide heterozygous fraction, 14 polar bodies
  median 0.1116, range 0.0937 to 0.2320, call rate 0.731 to 0.917

A polar body carries one parental genome, so its true heterozygous fraction is zero and every one
of those calls is error. That is the floor any ploidy statistic built on heterozygosity has to
clear on this material, and it is measured rather than assumed.

It also OVERLAPS the diploid range on this platform, where a bulk genome reads about 0.145 to 0.168
and single-cell diploids run lower still. So heterozygosity alone does not separate a polar body
from a diploid cell here, which is the same conclusion reached independently from the obligate-het
side and the reason a parental call needs the marker class rather than a global rate.

The PB1-versus-PB2 split could not be made from these records: the GEO titles number the polar
bodies within an embryo rather than by meiotic division, so which of a pair is 2C is not recoverable
without the laboratory's own annotation. That is the one question to ask, since a PB1 and PB2 pair
observes three chromatids of the tetrad and is worth substantially more than two independent
haploid products for reconstructing a mother.


THE RELATEDNESS BASELINE IS PLATFORM-SPECIFIC, AND CARRYING IT ACROSS WAS AN ERROR
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Relatedness above was judged against "unrelated adults sit near 0.30 opposite-homozygote", which is
correct on a dense common-SNP Illumina array and WRONG on GPL28377. Measured directly, on the full
825,657-marker exports:

  EYS sperm donor vs the 14801 cumulus mother   0.0727 over 552,595 shared homozygous markers

Those two are an unrelated donor couple, so 0.073 IS the unrelated baseline here. The array is
enriched for low-frequency variants - the sperm donor reads 0.163 heterozygous against the 0.30 of
a common-SNP panel - so both members of any pair are the same homozygote at most markers and
opposite homozygotes are intrinsically rare.

CONSEQUENCE. A cutoff of 0.12, chosen against the 0.30 benchmark, admits unrelated material.
Rechecking experiment 14801 against the correct baseline:

  14801-02  0.0012   her own genome
  14801-01  0.0155   14801-06  0.0195   14801-04  0.0214    her material
  14801-03  0.1141   ABOVE the 0.073 unrelated baseline, so NOT her child

So the 33-cell cohort used for the region work contains at least one array that is not this
mother's, and every cohort-null figure computed from it carries that contamination. The direction
of the effect is to widen the null and lose sensitivity, not to create false regions, which is
consistent with the low sensitivity measured throughout.

  Every relatedness threshold must be calibrated on the platform in use, from a pair known to be
  unrelated. There is one here: the sperm donor and the cumulus donor.

FORMAT NORMALISATION, CONFIRMED ACROSS THE THREE EXPORTS IN USE
----------------------------------------------------------------
Three shapes appear in this material and all three carry the same core columns, so one reader
covers them once the delimiter is not assumed:

  .CEL.probes            tab-separated,   7 columns   probeset_id chr position log2R baf
                                                      copy_number genotype
  .CEL.txt.gz  (GEO)     COMMA-separated, 8 columns   adds probe_classification and
                                                      normalized_intensity, drops log2R
  .subset.csv.gz (repo)  comma-separated, 6 columns   a marker subset of the above

probeset_id is the same AX- namespace in all three, so they join directly with no lift-over. The
GEO export omits log2R, which the dosage channel consumes, and carries normalized_intensity
instead; anything reading log2R must therefore fall back or refuse rather than assume the column.

CONFOUND 5: A LOSS CALLER THAT CANNOT SEPARATE COPY LOSS FROM DROPOUT
----------------------------------------------------------------------
[MECHANISM CORRECTED. This section first blamed the array's per-allele calling asymmetry. That was
measured afterwards, against meiosis, and is under one percent - see the calibration at the end of
this section. The result below is still not reportable; the cause is the loss caller.]
Run on the five biparental children of the bulk father, direction taken Mendelianly: at a marker
where the father is homozygous he can transmit only that allele, so a child carrying the OTHER
allele homozygously cannot have his copy. That is sound genetics and it produced:

  29 regions where a parental copy is lost.  PATERNAL 0.  MATERNAL 29.  binomial p < 0.0001.

It is wrong, and the control that shows it is the non-paternal fraction in regions with NO loss:

  cell         non-paternal in LOSS bins   in NORMAL bins
  32477-15               0.0048                0.0073
  22424-02               0.0268                0.0404
  22549-31               0.0209                0.0416
  A1                     0.0220                0.0311
  32477-10               0.0430                0.0420

The two are the same, and slightly LOWER where a copy is supposedly missing. The statistic that
assigns the parent therefore carries no information about the parent, and all 29 calls are
unsupported.

THE CALIBRATION, WHICH RULES OUT THE PLATFORM. Meiosis fixes the true answer at markers where the
father is HETEROZYGOUS: each of his gametes receives A or B with probability exactly one half, so
his haploid products measure the array against a known ratio with no biology in it. Over 127,826
such markers and four of his gametes:

  AA fraction 0.4948, 0.4945, 0.4880, 0.4964     mean 0.4934 against an expected 0.5000
  bias -0.0066, so the array calls A 0.974x as often as B when the truth is 1:1

Correcting an observed non-paternal fraction of 0.02 gives 0.0195. The bias is real, under one
percent, and explains none of the excess.

THE ACTUAL MECHANISM. At a father-AA marker most homozygous calls are legitimately AA, because the
mother transmitted A as well; the child is heterozygous only where she differs. A low non-paternal
fraction is therefore the EXPECTED baseline and not evidence of anything, which is exactly what the
loss-versus-normal control shows. The bins flagged as losses were amplification dropout rather than
copy loss, and carried no parental signal because nothing had been lost.

This remains the most dangerous confound in this file, because it produces the publishable-looking
result the study wants, at p < 0.0001, from five real children of a real genotyped father.

  WHAT IS REQUIRED BEFORE ANY SPLIT IS REPORTED. The per-allele asymmetry is now measured and is
  not the obstacle. What is needed is a loss caller that separates a real copy loss from
  amplification dropout, since the present one does not, and then parity reproduced on material
  where parity is known - the non-allele-specific Cas9 arm, if its guides are confirmed
  non-allele-specific. Until both hold, no parental split from this material is admissible,
  however small its p value.
